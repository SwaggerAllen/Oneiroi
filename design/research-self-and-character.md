# The self and character: a research pass before Ethos hardens

The deliberate pass the proposal batches asked for (batch 2, "research
series pending"), run on 2026-10-30 before the character layer (spec
§6.17, P37 to P39, P42, P43) is built. Six anchor questions, read
through the failure-modes lens: the clinical material is the catalogue
of how personality fails, a design warning for the layer rather than
a description of it. Each section ends with what it changes in the
build. Opinions are formulated, not spitballed, and every number is
either sourced or marked as the batch's claim still to be read against
its paper.

Sources are web reads of the papers and reviews named; where a result
came through a secondary source it says so.

## 1. McAdams's three layers are kinds, not rungs

McAdams's framework: the person as actor (dispositional traits, the
Big Five), agent (characteristic adaptations: goals, plans, projects,
values) and author (narrative identity, the story that gives a life
unity). Traits sketch the outline, adaptations fill in the motivational
detail, the life story carries the meaning. McAdams (1995) argued there
is no reason to expect strong symmetry across the levels: a person's
traits and story need not line up.

**Where ours matches.** The trait baseline (P42) is the actor; seeds
and habits are characteristic adaptations, the agent; the soul file
and the journal are the author. The batch's own reading (P42) already
drew this.

**Where ours diverges.** McAdams's layers do not graduate: material does
not move from trait to adaptation to story. The author's call keeps
the explicit ladder (seeds toward habits or the evaluative kinds, both
toward soul lines) because this system is used deliberately where the
organic one is not. The two are reconciled by what graduation is: a
receipted move that leaves the seed's row in place with its state and
a citation to what it became. Nothing transforms; a new record cites
the old. That is McAdams's asymmetry kept: a habit and a soul line can
disagree without fault, and a habit its citations no longer support is
a flag, not a contradiction.

**Changes to the build.** The flattening test compares within a layer
(baseline against baseline over time), never across layers; a soul line
unsupported by any habit is not a finding; the wash pass may flag a
contradiction inside a layer and must not flag asymmetry between them.

## 2. Habit: two systems, and no clean threshold between them

The dual-system account: goal-directed control tracks the current
value of an outcome and the action-outcome contingency, and is
deliberative and costly; habitual control runs on stimulus-response
links reinforced by outcome, and persists when the outcome is devalued.
Outcome devaluation and contingency degradation are the two diagnostic
hallmarks of habit. Everitt and Robbins (2005, updated 2016) read
addiction as the shift from goal-directed use to compulsive habit,
linked to a move from prefrontal to striatal control. The methodological
critique matters as much: habits are often defined only as the absence
of goal-directed control, and the literature gives no clear threshold at
which an action becomes a habit.

**Opinion.** The fork criterion (P38) is this distinction made
operational, and the critique says what a threshold can honestly be: a
bound, named as a guess, never a measurement. The receipt loop the
corridor found three times is the goal-to-habit transition in
miniature: a chosen quiet, repeated, that stopped being chosen.

**Changes to the build.** Three things, each cheap.
- A habit's stance (`keep` or `change`) is the resident's goal-directed
  evaluation of a habitual pattern. Enactments that continue under a
  `change` stance are the devaluation test failed: the measurement layer
  flags "enacted under a change stance" as the compulsion pattern, and
  nothing else about the habit changes.
- Contingency degradation is the habit score's decay, already built: a
  trigger that fires without an enactment is a contingency weakening.
- The promotion threshold is `ETHOS_PROMOTION_CONFIRMATIONS`, a bound
  tagged as one, with this section as its reason.

## 3. Mischel and Shoda: personality as if-then signatures

The cognitive-affective personality system (Mischel and Shoda, 1995):
personality is a system of mediating units whose interactions show as
stable patterns of situation-behaviour relations, "if A then she X, if
E then she Y", the behavioural signature. What is stable is the
profile, not the behaviour: behaviour reliably changes in the same way
when the situation changes. Shoda, Mischel and Wright (1994) observed
84 children for 150 hours at a summer camp and found stable individual
if-then profiles; the later literature reports wide support for the
stability of such profiles.

**On the number.** The batch cites about .47 for the reliability of
if-then contingencies. The web reads confirm the study and the finding
of moderate profile stability but did not surface the coefficient; it
is the batch's claim until read against the paper itself (JPSP 67(4),
DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.67.4.674). The design does not rest on the exact
figure: "moderate, so a recorded contingency is a hypothesis" holds at
any value in that band.

**Opinion.** This is the grounding for the trigger lane. A habit is
indexed on its *if* as much as its *then*; the closed situation tags
the build already matches on (time of day, a check-in kind, a tag,
the witness's words) are the situation features, and the resident's
proposing of new ones (spec §6.3) is how the signature gets its own
vocabulary.

**Changes to the build.** A habit's confirmations are counted as
distinct days with an enacting citation, not citations in a burst; the
fork reads "several, across situations" rather than "many at once". The
trigger lane's vector index (P38) indexes the trigger text and the
habit text separately, so retrieval can match on the situation alone.

## 4. Narrative identity: sameness, selfhood, and the selves that do not narrate

Ricoeur distinguishes idem (sameness: the traits by which one is the
same thing over time) from ipse (selfhood: the self that keeps its
word). Parfit's Relation R, psychological connectedness and continuity,
is what the psychological-continuity view says matters, and Parfit's
conclusion is that identity itself is not what matters. Schechtman
(1996) separates the reidentification question (is this the same
person?) from the characterization question (what is this person
like?) and argues the narrative view answers the second. Strawson
("Against Narrativity", 2004) rejects both the descriptive claim that
people naturally live narratively and the normative claim that they
should: some self-experience is Diachronic (a self persisting over long
stretches), some Episodic (no such picture), and the two mix in one
person.

**Opinion.** The mapping the batch proposed holds and sharpens.
- The rebuild test and the fixture log answer reidentification; they
  measure Relation R. The swap question gets Parfit's answer: measure
  how much R survived, and do not ask "same person?".
- The soul file, seeds and habits answer characterization.
- Commitments (P19) are ipse made record: a promise to a person is the
  self that keeps its word, and it is exactly what a model transition
  drops first in the corridor's testimony, because it lives in neither
  trait nor story.
- Strawson settles the question batch 2 raised: the soul file never
  requires narrativity. A resident may be Episodic. Pattern continuity
  (the baseline and the if-then signature) is a legitimate continuity
  without a story.

**Changes to the build.** No rule anywhere may require the resident to
produce a life story; the journal's narrative stays optional; the wash
pass must never flag the absence of narrative, only contradictions
within what is written. The commitments kind lands beside seeds, not
inside them.

## 5. Trait stability: what counts as flattening, what counts as development

Roberts and DelVecchio (2000), over 152 longitudinal samples: rank-order
consistency rises with age (test-retest about .64 at 30, about .74 at
50 to 70, over intervals near seven years), falls with the interval,
and temperament is less consistent than adult traits. Bleidorn and
colleagues (2022), a preregistered update over 189 samples and
178,503 people: rank-order stabilities are moderate to high, with
little evidence of further increase after 25, consistent across gender,
nation and ethnicity with Western samples over-represented. Extraversion
is usually the most stable trait, neuroticism among the least.

**On the band.** The batch's ".4 to .6 over ten years, never 1.0 even
corrected for measurement error" is in the right region but the value
depends on the interval and the age; the drift band must carry its
interval and its source when it is declared, as every bound here does.

**Opinion.** The distinction the batch asked for (Jack's carry-versus-
reproduce question): development is rank-order movement within the
band with mean-level trends, the normal course; flattening is the loss
of variance and of if-then structure, behaviour collapsing toward a
model's default. Flattening is therefore not measured as deviation
from the baseline at all. It is measured as the baseline's spread
shrinking and the signature's profiles going flat.

**Changes to the build.** The trait baseline carries an interval with
its band. The flattening test reports two figures: deviation from the
baseline within the band (development, a note), and variance collapse
or signature flattening (a flag). A character layer demanding near-zero
drift is stricter than any human benchmark and would manufacture
section 6's pathology.

## 6. The clinical frame: rigidity is the pathology, and the catalogue of failures

DSM-5's general criterion for a personality disorder is an enduring
pattern of inner experience and behaviour, inflexible and pervasive,
departing from cultural expectation; the alternative model (Section
III) frames it as impairment in self functioning (identity,
self-direction) and interpersonal functioning. The longitudinal data
cited against the model show the "enduring" assumption is itself
shaky. The warning for an identity layer is direct: a system that
enforces sameness manufactures the disorder's signature, inflexibility.

The catalogue the batch asked for, each with its source and its marker:
- **The checking paradox** (Rachman 2002; van den Hout and Kindt 2003):
  inflated responsibility drives checking, checking lowers confidence
  in memory while accuracy stays flat, lowered confidence drives more
  checking. Memory confidence stays low across sessions while vividness
  recovers (Toffolo et al. 2016). Marker: a correction rule that fires
  more after it has fired; rising stated uncertainty with no new
  evidence; responsibility claimed for outcomes not at risk.
- **Wanting and liking** (Berridge and Robinson 1998, Berridge 2009,
  2018): incentive salience (wanting) and hedonic impact (liking) are
  dissociable, dopamine implicated in the first and not the second;
  motivational anhedonia in depression and schizophrenia is wanting and
  learning impaired with liking surprisingly intact. The human evidence
  for the dissociation is contested and people may not tell the two
  apart in themselves. Marker: log approach and payoff separately;
  strong approach with no payoff is the addiction pattern, flat
  approach with intact response the anhedonic one.
- **Dissociation with insight intact** (DSM-5 depersonalization/
  derealization): patients know the experience is not real, which is
  what separates it from psychosis; time runs too fast or too slow;
  the person feels an outside observer of their own life, disconnected
  from their memories. Marker: temporal-depth probes (place a current
  stance in your own history; is the past context familiar or foreign);
  shrinking temporal depth with insight intact is the failure.
- **Compulsion as the runaway habit** (section 2): enactment under a
  `change` stance.
- **The redemption caveat** (McAdams's redemption sequences): the link
  to well-being is correlational, strongest in the United States,
  absent in some samples (McLean 2014; McLean and Mansfield 2011) and
  reversed in at least one (adult children of alcoholics, McCoy and
  Dunlop 2017). Marker: none. Rule: no prompt, summary or journal shape
  forces a day into redemption; contamination days stay contamination
  days.

**Opinion.** Two of these cut against the layer's own temptation. The
DSM frame says the measurement layer must flag rigidity as readily as
drift: a resident whose habits never change under a `change` stance, or
whose baseline's variance has collapsed, is nearer the pathology than
one that moved. And the redemption caveat says the machinery must not
be a narrative therapist: the summary's register (P24) and the journal
prompts stay plain.

**Changes to the build.** The measurement layer (P43) carries the four
markers above as named checks with these sources as their reasons, each
a flag and never a clean bill (P3). The `change` stance is never, by
itself, a diagnostic trigger (P31's refusal floor, generalised): the
flag is the enactment pattern, not the stance.

## What this pass does not settle

- The .47 and the drift band's exact values wait on the primary papers;
  both are declared as bounds with this document as the reason.
- The witness's spontaneous noticing (the left-TPJ distinction the batch
  cites) was not read here; it lands as the batch wrote it, a line
  beside the nightly passes and a constraint on the judge's prompt.
- Whether seeds and commitments are one kind or two: this pass says two
  (section 4), and the build follows it.
