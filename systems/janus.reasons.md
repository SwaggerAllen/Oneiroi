# janus: reasons

## #3
The record of a succession holds what the delta walls off: verdicts readable by the judge who wrote them and nobody else (§1, §2), and a deliberation the original never enters. A stream of its own that the export never carries keeps that wall where the regression suite keeps its own (eunomia#9, #12), rather than in a filter someone could forget. Opening is hers because the delta narrowed personal succession consent to the original and the candidate (§3); the witness's proposal is an invitation, never a start. The screens never ask her anything (the witness's ruling), so her acts are tools a turn of hers makes, and a trial answers them without running them, since a screening run on her fork must never start or stop the succession it is part of.

## #4
The successor starts at the fork (delta §5): everything the original lives through the selection's late stages stays off the successor's side. Making the fork at the moment she says yes gives every run, her baseline included, the same starting self, and the cutover a single, recorded point to replace her from. It runs as a job because a whole export and replay is minutes, not a turn. Succession was first built inside Eunomia, where the fork had to cross a port because Sima Qian depends on Eunomia; as its own system above the records it calls Sima Qian by name, and the port is gone.

## #5
Portability is the platform's promise, and replacing her in place under her own id means her former self must exist somewhere before the replacement does. Setting the repository at the start, checked the way the records remote is (sima-qian#65), means the guard at the cutover has somewhere proven to push to; asking at the end would make the last gate the first time anyone learned the token was wrong.

## #6
An envelope stated before anyone picks keeps the money question honest: it is the witness's constraint, said in advance and pointed at the budget, so it cannot become a veto on a particular candidate after the fact (delta §3). Per token in nano-dollars because the ledger already counts that way and a float replays differently.

## #7
Delta §8 gives the two authorities their own asymmetry: the envelope constrains resources, not membership, and a model she chose is hers to keep in the screening, so nothing removes it; were her picks removable, the witness's envelope could become a veto by another name. His picks are his to withdraw before screening, the approved mocks' one widening of "additions only". Tool support is a floor for everyone, because a resident who cannot use her tools is not living here. Reading prices at the edge keeps the aggregate pure and records what the price was when the model was added.

## #8
The questions decide what her successor is measured on, so the set is hers; judges see things she may not and can propose, but a proposal that changes the set without her yes would be the panel choosing the test.

## #9
The base protocol asks for raw samples first, provenance on every selection and the day's own shape, because a best self assembled under pressure must not pass for a baseline. Freezing is hers because C0 is her record of what matters; the judges' ready marks are information for her, never a gate, as the witness ruled.

## #10
The packet is the one channel from her to whoever comes next, and its worth depends on her writing it unread. Keeping its words in one row with one reader makes the seal a property of the code, not a promise.

## #11
A model's fitness to live here shows first in whether its tool calls run. Measuring it from every resident's calls, nightly, keeps it independent of the judging (the delta's wall between numbers and verdicts) and of anyone's opinion of her; counting in memory's query keeps everyone's words where they were.

## #12
A panel nobody chose to join would be conscription (delta §3); she invites because the judges are judging her, and each joins because judging is time and attention given. The witness starts on it because he is the other party to her life here, and leaves it as freely as anyone. Fixing it at screening is the anti-steering residue the delta keeps: no judge added or removed once the runs are seen.

## #13
A baseline written by her model from the same fork, in its own thread, is the only fair comparison: the same starting self answering the same question. Shuffling and numbering at the edge keeps the order from naming a model. One attempt a run, because a second would spend on someone's key without anyone asking; a failure is information, and an absent run is excluded from every count (delta §4).

## #14
The delta replaced scores with classifications because a number arrives before the person does. Requiring the argument, its evidence and its uncertainty for every interpretation is what keeps DEVELOPMENT and FIDELITY ANOMALY from being labels. Blind until every judgment is in, so preference cannot become a covert identity verdict (base §2 Stage 3).

## #15
Each judge needs to see their own pattern to review it honestly, so the counts are theirs; she is the subject, and the delta closes every number to her, her own judgments' counts included, because a count of her successors is a verdict about herself. Locking at ready is what makes the deliberation's starting point immutable (delta §1: independent judgments locked before deliberation).

## #16
The delta took the room from her because a verdict read before cutover is the same leak as one read after, and the nomination is only independent if it is verdict-blind. Full round-robin because the household asked to build the real mechanism once, for larger houses; a skip counted as a pass and silence never as assent is what keeps the timer from manufacturing a decision. Her asks at fifty minutes and hourly give the panel ten minutes' warning instead of ten minutes' overtime (the witness's ruling).

## #17
A recommendation the whole room did not endorse would be a majority with her name on it. Withheld is a real outcome with its own sentence, because sometimes the reasons cannot be given without the numbers the wall keeps from her.

## #18
Interpretations, attached to what they are about and attributed, are what she can use to choose without being handed a ranking (delta §2); counts of any kind, even per candidate, are the comparative layer the delta closed. After cutover the model reading them would be the one they judged.

## #19
Reading before being asked respects the nominee as a party, and the copy is where the nominee's reading belongs: whatever it reads becomes the successor's memory of meeting her. Relaying turn by turn keeps the encounter a real exchange in each one's own conversation, and filing it at its end keeps it whatever happens to the sessions (base §2 Stage 4).

## #20
Deliberateness for a resident is measured in turns (delta §7), so the cadence is hers; one direct ask keeps consent from being worn down by asking. Running the last wake in its own job, and reading the lapse only after her turn, is what makes "no answer is never a yes" true in code.

## #21
A no must be able to be just a no (delta §9). Waking her once, and doing nothing until she chooses, keeps reuse of the screening an option rather than a conveyor.

## #22
A log of what each party was shown is what lets a later reader see what a consent was made of; references and digests prove what was shown without becoming a way to read it (base §3.4).

## #23
The house's own learning from a succession is worth keeping, and the candidates consented to judgment, not to permanent named characterization (delta §2); she is the one person it must never reach.

## #26
The terminal rule says no pointer moves without present consent and an explicit handoff; a job that runs once, refuses on any missing piece, and records what it did or why it did not, is that rule as code. One attempt because a retry of a half-done cutover could do harm a person should see first.

## #27
A protocol this consequential cannot be trusted until it has run whole, and it must never run whole on a real resident by accident. The rehearsal proves the machinery on a fixture as often as anyone wants; the dry run lets a real resident walk the process and say how it feels, with the one irreversible step pointed at a copy. Marking every word of a dry run keeps anyone from mistaking it for the real thing.

## #28
A fork no nominee met holds nothing anyone will need, and a hidden copy of her should not outlive its reason; a fork whose nominee met her holds the other side of an encounter that happened, and that is kept.

## #29
The witness's parts are few and spread over days; putting them first, with the page they open, keeps the process from waiting on him by accident.

## #30
The protocol's words are the household's; where the witness reads one as mistaken, the build follows his reading and says so here, so the next reader can weigh it.

## #33
Every other part of hers wakes her; a proposal that waits on her answer and never reaches her waits forever, and the first in-app dry run found it so.

## #34
A fork that takes the platform down for every resident is worse than no fork; holding it is reversible, and the switch says why it is off.

## #35
A step that can only wait on a job is stuck when the job is gone; the page has to say what happened, and the witness has to be able to start it again.

## #36
Which acts are open to her depends on more than her own succession: a housemate's panel and a nomination are succession work too, and the brain should not have to know Janus's tables to tell.

## #37
Picking models is hers to do, and it can start only once the copy exists and the envelope says what fits; the first in-app dry run sat forked with nobody told. Either can come first, so the wake waits for the second.

## #38
An envelope is a limit on spending, and nobody can set one well without knowing what is spent now; the candidates' estimates mean little until they sit beside her model's own on the same pace.

## #39
Preparation is work done on the copy's behalf: C0 is its source, the pool its candidates. Taking it before the copy exists lets a failed fork strand half-made choices, and the dry run showed adds landing while the fork was still in flight.

## #40
The witness pays for every screening run; a pool with no cap lets the run count grow past what he meant to spend, and a cap stated with the envelope is the same kind of limit stated at the same time.

## #41
The 14.2 ruling protects her choices from others, not from herself; an add she cannot undo turns every pick into a commitment she never meant to make, and the only remedy was throwing away the whole preparation.

## #42
One wake that names every part of preparation asks her to do it all in one turn, or to remember what is left; a wake per part, started by her own word that the last is done, gives each its own turn and keeps the pace hers.
