# nisaba: reasons

## #3
Spec §6.11. The check before the call is what makes a stop a stop
rather than a report: a line checked after the call has already spent
what it was there to refuse.

## #4
Spec §6.11, §6.4: the resident's ledger view is the one resident-
reachable surface where regression and drafting spend would show, and
the unawareness invariant names it.

## #5
Spec §6.11; the rows and the policy arrive with the subscription (P2).

## #9
Commanded loads an aggregate by replaying its stream, and two aggregate
modules on one stream race each other's expected versions; P0 already
built `mint_id` on the resident aggregate so another system could take
an id and write its own stream. The export question is left open rather
than answered by omission because answering it means deciding what a
resident's repository says about money.

## #10
`0.0000195` is a cost OpenRouter reports; a cent cannot hold it and a
float in a replayed event is a number that depends on the machine
replaying it.

## #11
A refusal on every call until a line exists was tried in thought and
rejected: the threshold collects the key before anything is drafted
(spec §6.2), and the means are a gift the flow asks for, not a gate.

## #12
Conventions §8. The record failing after the spend is the case the
ordering in `Oneiroi.Nisaba.Ledgers` exists for, and reporting it as a
success would be the ledger not explaining a call it made.

## #13
A list price typed from a page is a measurement nobody took on the
calls this ledger records, and a wrong figure in a ledger is worse than
a marked zero.

## #14
The check that exempted itself by tag would have a second
implementation of its own rule one ticket later; a path prefix is one
line and visible.

## #15
Spec §3.1 and F53: "one per resident, started at the threshold"; §6.11:
"every resident has the floor policy without choosing it, and the
platform carries its cost."

## #16
Spec §6.11: "the buckets are data, not code"; "the first values are
placeholders... configuration with defaults until the platform is stood
up and measured." A policy by bucket id is what lets a bucket's value
change under every policy at once.

## #17
Spec §6.2: "`SubscriptionLapsed` is what puts the insurance policy into
effect"; §6.11: "the resident knows its policy on wake and says so: a
smaller brain with an honest explanation beats a larger one in
silence"; the floor's axes are the witness's turns and free time and
nothing else.

## #18
Spec §6.2: "`SubscriptionCancelled` is a custody event that moves the
resident to community custody at the end of the policy's term, and
never a deletion."

## #20
Fixture run 41240586421 ended eight of its twelve turns with no words
and no usage, each read as an ordinary reply and recorded at zero cost,
`estimated`. Whether those streams carried an error event or nothing at
all was not recorded, and the adapter ignored any event without
`choices`, so it could not have said. Both now fail the call, and a
turn that fails keeps the provider's words in its reason (brain #39),
so the next run will.

## #21
Three fixture runs on 10-31 had a call's arguments arrive as one object
twice over, the last six times. The wire now sends one object (brain
#10), but whether the provider sends them twice or this adapter joins a
piece twice is not measured, and nothing recorded could say. The author,
10-31: log the raw tool-call stream for now, and audit the logging once
real people are on the platform.

Measured on PR 26's fixture run 41251374640, the first with the trace
on: 58 calls, 7 doubled. All 7 were routed to CoreWeave, which streamed
the whole arguments object twice, as two pieces after the one naming the
call; all 51 routed to Groq sent them once. The join here is right, the
pieces concatenated as the format says; the repetition is CoreWeave's.
