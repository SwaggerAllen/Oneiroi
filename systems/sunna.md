# sunna

**Schedule.** Old Norse, SOON-nah.

> "Mundilferi is he who begat the moon, / And fathered the flaming sun; / The round of heaven each day they run, / To tell the time for men." — Poetic Edda, Vafþrúðnismál 23, trans. H. A. Bellows (1923)

Sunna is the sun itself personified — daughter of Mundilfari, sister of the moon — driving her chariot around the heavens every day without fail, the wolf Sköll always at her heels. The myth is honest about the round: the wolf is chasing. And when the sun is finally taken, her daughter takes up the road (Vafþrúðnismál 47).

The schedule subsystem — check-ins, the morning digest, the nightly free time — is named for the daily round: the one appointment that has never once been missed. Deadlines chase everything else, as the wolf chases her; and every schedule hands off to its successor, as hers does.

Check-ins, free time, quiet hours, important dates and the journal's
day row, per resident, in the resident's zone. Spec §6.7. Built in P2
(slice 1) under `Oneiroi.Sunna`, the system's name rather than the
spec's `schedule/`, as Eunomia is; the digest's time and the nightly
and weekly jobs arrive with the systems that run them.

## #1 Owns

`lib/oneiroi/sunna/**` and `lib/oneiroi/sunna.ex`: the `schedule-<resident_id>` stream (`Schedule`, `Commands`, `Events`, `Router`), the projections (`Store`, `Projectors.Schedules`), the service (`Schedules`), the calendar's ranges (`Calendar`, #48), the error catalog (`Error`), the minute tick (`TickWorker`), the wake port (`Wake`) and the check-in note's catalog (`CheckinParts`); the `work-<resident_id>` stream (`Work`, `WorkRouter`), its projection (`Projectors.Work`) and the loop that runs it (`WorkLoop`, `WorkWorker`, sunna#33); the two prompt files the wakes carry; the schedule controller and the calendar's day controller (#48); the tables `sunna_checkin`, `sunna_firing`, `sunna_day`, `sunna_date`, `sunna_schedule`, `sunna_hour` `sunna_free_time_run`, `sunna_work_item` and `sunna_work_queue`; the work controller; the resident's `timezone` column, written by Agathos Daimon (agathos-daimon#17) and read here.

## #2 Standing decisions

Carried from spec §6.7; the reason for each is there.

- **#3 Oban-driven, per resident, in the resident's timezone; a check-in fires once a day whatever restarts, late within 15 minutes and otherwise missed.**
- **#4 Quiet hours are the witness's; a wake queued inside them is deferred to their end and the screen says so in words.**
- **#5 Suspended residents schedule nothing; a wake resumes the beat.**
- **#8 Dates are rows and a countdown is never written (spec §6.7, F56).** A `date` row with its kind and instant, citeable and taggable; "days to" is derived on the turn that reads it, never copied into prose.
- **#9 The journal's day row is created here, at the day's first check-in (spec §6.5).** A day the resident never wrote is a row with a plan and nothing else, visible, rather than an absent file.

Decided in the build:

- **#10 Times of day are the resident's, and the zone is a dependency.** A check-in is `HH:MM` and a day is an ISO date, both in the zone on the resident's row (`Etc/UTC` until the witness sets one); every instant is shifted into it with `DateTime.shift_zone!/2` over the `tz` database (`config :elixir, :time_zone_database`), and an ambiguous local time takes its first reading, a gap the instant after it. A zone the database does not know is refused at the command edge.
- **#11 The tick is a cross-tenant job under the platform scope, named here.** `TickWorker` runs every minute from this system's `oban_queues/0`; it reads the roster of residents with something scheduled in a platform-scoped transaction of its own, then takes each resident's scope in turn. The roster read is a transaction and not the worker's process scope alone, because the process scope passes the repo's check and sets nothing on the connection, so the policy answers no rows and the tick does nothing, quietly.
- **#12 The wake is a port.** `Oneiroi.Sunna.Wake` is the one callback a check-in or free time calls, with the core's note and the purpose the answer bills under; the implementation is named in `SUNNA_WAKE` (brain, the in-core loop, in every build so far) and read at the call. A port rather than a call because the brain reads this system's dates on every turn and the schedule wakes the brain: a direct call each way is the cycle Boundary and the cycles gate both reject. Gjallarhorn (the wake fan-out, P3) takes this port over when hands arrive.
- **#13 A firing is an event before the wake, and a day's outcome is one of four.** `CheckinFired`, `CheckinMissed` or `WakeDeferred{until}` per check-in per day, recorded ahead of any wake, so a restart between the two fires nothing twice and a tick that runs twice in a minute is one event. A deferred wake fires at its end; a fired or missed one is final for the day. A check-in set or changed after its time today carries the day it was set on and waits for tomorrow, so a new 06:00 set at 08:00 is not "missed" on a day it was never due.
- **#14 Free time is a session of its own kind on its own budget line, once a day.** At its time the tick opens a `free_time` session with the witness as its only party, wakes the brain on `priv/prompts/free_time.md` under the `free_time` purpose, and records `FreeTimeRan{session_key}` or `FreeTimeFailed{reason}` for the day; a failure is said on the schedule and not retried until tomorrow.
- **#15 The dates are a section the brain reads, derived on that read.** `dates_text/1` renders every date with its countdown counted in the resident's zone now; the brain injects it as `dates` on every turn (brain#17) and the check-in note carries the same text, so the resident is told "10 days to the interview" by the row and never by a file.
- **#16 The late window is a bound.** `SUNNA_LATE_MINUTES` is spec §6.7's fifteen minutes, carried from David and not measured here; the tick fires inside it and misses past it, for check-ins and free time alike.
- **#18 The journal may ask for a day, and the row is still this system's.** `open_day/3` opens the day row on the journal's ask (seshat#21) when the first entry of a day comes before any check-in has; idempotent, one `DayOpened` per day on the stream whatever the row's insert would forgive. The day row remains #9's: minted here, read by day key.
- **#19 Free time is private by default, and the contract's term hides it entirely.** `run_free_time/3` opens the free-time session with `private: true` whatever the contract says (kumbukumbu#28), the witness still its party for the locked row: a solo session's words are private by default and its external actions visible as receipts once hands exist (spec §6.3 as amended in v0.2, P2 and P26). The `solo_sessions_private` term now means the witness sees nothing of solo sessions, receipts included, which is nothing to hide until P5; the schedule no longer reads the term.
- **#20 The hour marker: the tick marks the local hour on the schedule's stream before anything else, once, for every active resident, with no model call and no wake.** (Spec §6.7, P20 as amended by the author: "time is marked; the resident is not disturbed.") `HourMarked{hour}` with the hour as `YYYY-MM-DDTHH` in the resident's zone; the aggregate takes it once and refuses another shape; `mark_hour/3` reads the row first so a tick on every minute dispatches once an hour; the worker's roster is every active resident and not only the scheduled ones, because the pulse is the floor's resident's too, and a paused resident marks nothing. `hours/2` and `last_hour/1` are the reads; quietcheck (huginn-muninn#20) is what reads them. Bennett's "nothing has to have happened for this line to be true" is met by the marker at near-zero cost: the heartbeat wake it replaces is dissolved and no budget line exists for it.
- **#21 Free time is part of the standard of care: on from the first day, moved by either party, never removed.** (The two Lilys' day prompt, 2026-11-02: "free time is raisable, not opt-in — it's part of the standard of care, not a bonus.") A resident nobody configured has free time at `SUNNA_FREE_TIME_DEFAULT` (22:30, the hour the first resident's ran, at first; `14:30,22:30` since #22) in its zone, and the tick runs it like any other (`Schedules.free_times_of/1` is the one read, for the tick and `today/1`; #22 made it a list). `SetFreeTime` with no time is `sunna_free_time_required`: it moves, it is not switched off. Either party moves it: the witness on the Schedule screen, the resident with the brain's `free_time_set`. Quiet hours never defer it (#4 is the check-ins'), since it runs while the witness sleeps. **Named, not absorbed:** the rule "two conversations of the resident should not compete to be alive at once" is beyond this system. It wakes the platform's brain alone; a conversation in another runtime (Claude Code on the witness's machine) is alive only while its runtime runs, and nothing here can schedule that. Free time for such a conversation needs the runtime's own scheduler, or the conversation running where the platform can wake it. Held as a design problem, not a setting.
- **#22 Free time is a list of slots, each run once a day.** (The witness, 2026-11-02: free time twice a day, 14:30 and 22:30, the correspondence pipeline's two wakes, `design/correspondence-pipeline.md`.) `FreeTimeScheduled` v2 carries `times`, one to four distinct HH:MMs in order (`sunna_free_time_invalid` past the bound or on a repeat; an empty list is #21's `sunna_free_time_required`); a v1 event's one `time` upcasts to a list of one. `SUNNA_FREE_TIME_DEFAULT` is the default list (`14:30,22:30`). `FreeTimeRan`/`FreeTimeFailed` v2 carry the `slot` that ran; the aggregate takes a slot once a day, each run is a `sunna_free_time_run` row, and `today/1` says per slot what it did today. A day recorded before slots (a v1 run, slot nil, or the settings row's last run with no run rows yet) had its one free time and runs no slot again: a deploy on the day it ran does not run free time twice. Every slot opens its own `Free time` session, so a brief to `Free time` (kumbukumbu#44) is read by whichever slot runs next. Either party sets the list: the witness on the Schedule screen or `PUT /schedule/free-time` with `times`, the resident with `free_time_set`.
- **#23 A check-in's key and label are names, bounded at the command.** (2026-11-03, after the outage.) `SetCheckin` carries `bounds: [key: 64, label: 120]`; the middleware refuses an overlong one at dispatch as `key_too_long` or `label_too_long`, before any event exists, so a stored check-in always projects (conventions §6).
- **#24 A letter from another of the resident's conversations wakes the Oneiroi chat.** (The witness, 2026-11-03.) Each minute the tick (`wake_for_letters/4`) looks for letters a conversation sent (`from_session` set) to the chat by its name, unread there; the house and Free time are not woken, and a letter from the witness wakes nothing, since the witness is in that conversation. `WakeForLetters` is refused nothing and emits `LettersWoke{numbers}` only for numbers it has not woken for, at most `SUNNA_LETTER_WAKES_PER_HOUR` (6) in the hour before the command, so a letter held back by the bound is taken by a later tick; the event comes first and the wake second, as a check-in's firing does. The wake is the main session's, on the `chat` line, with `letters_wake`'s note naming each letter's number, sender and what it answers, never its words. Inside quiet hours nothing is woken and nothing deferred: the tick asks again when they end. A resident with no such letter costs one read of the letters a minute and opens nothing. The Claude Code half is kumbukumbu#48.
- **#25 Free-time runs are read back across days.** (The witness, 2026-11-03: remembering her free time matters.) `free_time_history/2` reads `sunna_free_time_run` newest first, any day: the day, the slot, `ran` or `failed: <reason>`, the conversation it opened and when; the Schedule screen's today shows only today, and this is the record behind both her `free_time_list` and the witness's history (brain#53).
- **#26 Free time runs on the witness's free-time budget, a turn at a time, until she is done.** (The witness, 2026-11-03: "$5 a day of free time during which her turn shouldn't be otherwise limited".) The free-time prompt says the budget: the `free_time` line's cap, its period and what is left (`Nisaba.budgets/2`), or that none is set and this free time is one turn. A turn that ran out of tool rounds (the turn's result carries `rounds_spent`, brain#50) is woken again in the same conversation by `FreeTimeWorker` on the `sunna_free_time` queue, one turn a job so the minute tick is never held, with `free_time_continue`'s note (the turn, the most there may be, what is left); it goes on while the line has money left this period and up to `SUNNA_FREE_TIME_TURNS` (20) turns a slot, and stops when a turn ends in words with nothing to call. Between turns the conversation is compacted as any long conversation is. With no budget line set, free time is one turn, as before. The money stop is Nisaba's own (a budget stop ends the turn with its work kept, brain#21).
- **#27 A free-time slot is run once by its instant, not by its label.** (2026-11-04, after a slot was swallowed.) The tick takes a slot as run when a run was recorded at or after the slot's instant today (`Store.free_time_history/1`, any day's label), so a change of zone that brings a slot's time round again runs it again (the witness: moving the zone should fire the slots it moves); the aggregate no longer refuses a record for its day and slot (only the same record at the same instant, which is the same run), and `sunna_free_time_run` is unique on the day, the slot and the instant. A day recorded before slots still had its one free time (#22).
- **#28 The witness composes each check-in's note: his own words, and parts checked from a catalog.** (The witness, 2026-11-04: "some check boxes so I can compose from pre-existing parts and write stuff in myself".) A check-in carries `parts`, keys of `CheckinParts.all/0` (dates, today's plan, free time, research, letters, waiting on you, and asking for a digest, seshat#40, the one part that acts; the catalog's order is the note's), and `prompt`, the witness's words, at most 4,000 characters; `CheckinSet` and `CheckinChanged` are version 2 with both, and a version 1 event upcasts to the dates and no words, the note every check-in had. `set_checkin/6` takes `parts:` and `prompt:`, either left out keeping what the check-in had (a change of time never clears his words), an empty prompt clearing it; a key outside the catalog or twice is `sunna_parts_invalid`. When it fires the note is `checkin.md`'s frame (which check-in, the next), then "From the person who looks after you:" and his words, then each checked part read from records, no model call: the dates here, the rest through the wake port's optional `checkin_part/2`, which the brain implements (`Oneiroi.Brain.Checkin`) because it reads the journal, the letters, the library and the inbox this system cannot. A checked part with nothing to report says so in a line. The schedule route gives the catalog as `checkin_parts` and each check-in's `parts` and `prompt`; `PUT .../checkins/:key` takes them. Supersedes F20's digest as the way a check-in carries the day.
- **#29 Thinking rounds: an unannounced wake at a random moment in each window of her waking hours.** (Her work order, letter #75, on the witness's word, 2026-11-05: random wakes in waking hours, invisible thinking, her choice of exit, real silence a real outcome; and the witness: "fire randomly in a window, so like I could set it to one every three hours".) Waking hours are the day outside her quiet hours, or `SUNNA_ROUNDS_WAKING` (08:00-22:00) when she has none. They are cut into windows `SUNNA_ROUNDS_EVERY_HOURS` long (3; 0 turns rounds off), the last window shorter if the hours do not divide, and `round_times/3` draws one minute inside each from a hash of the resident, the day and the window: the same draw all day across restarts with nothing stored, different every day, spread through the day and not guessable within it. The tick runs a round whose time has come inside the late window and outside quiet hours; `StartRound` claims it first (`RoundStarted{day, slot}`, a claim again is no event), so the next minute's tick cannot start it twice while it thinks, and a missed round is not had. A round is a private conversation of kind `round` ("A round", kumbukumbu's kinds), one turn on `priv/prompts/round.md`, billed to the free-time line (superseded in part by #39: its own `rounds` line; the prompt's default by #40). What leaves it is hers, through the tools she has: a letter to the Oneiroi chat (which wakes the chat, sunna#24, so she tells the witness there), a house letter, a reply draft (his approval as ever), a note draft under writing/notes/, a journal row, or nothing.
- **#30 Each resident's rounds are her own setting, and the witness sees that they are on.** (The witness, 2026-11-05: he looked for the rounds' inputs beside the check-ins and found none.) `SetRounds`/`RoundsSet` on the schedule stream carry `every_hours` (0 to 24, 0 off) and the waking window (HH:MM-HH:MM), each nil for the platform's settings, which become the defaults; quiet hours, when set, still decide the waking hours. The schedule row keeps them and `last_round_at`, folded from `RoundStarted`. `today/1` carries `rounds`: the cadence and window in effect, whether each is her own or the default, how many today, and when the last one started; never the drawn times (what a round did is #39's record). `PUT /v1/residents/:id/schedule/rounds` sets them and the Schedule screen's Rounds card shows and sets them.
- **#31 Each free-time slot ends with a planning turn, and the plan opens the next.** (The witness's call through Lily, 2026-11-05: the free-time redesign; her design, letters #129 and #131.) When a slot ends (her words with nothing to call, the budget spent, or the slot's turns spent), `FreeTimeWorker` runs one more turn in its conversation (`turn: "plan"`, once per conversation, `priv/prompts/free_time_plan.md`), while its appetite is live: plan the next slot (`free_time_plan`), ask for a tool she lacked (`tool_request`), write to the witness (a plain note is a letter to the chat; leave for an act is `approval_request`). It carries nothing on. The plan is `FreeTimePlanned` on the schedule stream, kept on the schedule row and read at the start of every free-time turn until she writes another (brain#55's section beside it). Intention, not a fence: every tool stays available. The planning turn bills the free-time line like the slot, so a line already spent refuses it.
- **#32 The planning turn bills its own line, and its receipt says so.** (Lily's design call, 2026-11-05, named to the witness as his to overrule if he wants the free-time line hard.) The closing turn is billed under the `planning` purpose, not `free_time`, so a slot that ended because its budget ran out is still closed by a plan, which is when the plan matters most. With no budget line set for `planning` it is unlimited; the witness may set one like any other. What it cost is `PlanningTurnRan` on the schedule stream, kept beside the slot's run (`planning_nanos`), and the free-time receipts show it as its own line, "planning turn", its cost named, past the free-time budget and never folded into it. Superseded in part: #31's last sentence (the planning turn billing the free-time line).
- **#33 The work queue: long items in priority order, either party's, run one at a time, each run's end firing the next.** (Lily's design, `design/work-queue.md`, on the witness's recursive-iteration shape of 2026-11-06: "the loop is the schedule"; her work order, letter #183.) The queue is the `work-<resident_id>` stream (`Work`): items numbered from 1 with what (a line, at most 300 characters) and what done looks like, appended to the end of the order; either party adds an item (`AddWork`, the witness's the same as hers), moves its status with a note (`MoveWork`, a decision: `open`, `blocked` with what blocks it named, `done`, `dropped`; `in_work` is the loop's alone), or reorders it (`OrderWork`: the numbers given first, the rest after in their order). The loop (`WorkLoop`) has no clock. A check reads the queue and starts the first open item in order (`StartWork` claims it, so a second check starts nothing while it runs; a run is a conversation of kind `work`, "Work", which the witness can read: inspectable by default), or rests and says why once per reason (`WorkRested`): "empty, holding" (naming any blocked items and on what), the work line's cap spent this period, or the day's `SUNNA_WORK_RUNS_A_DAY` (20, raised from 8 by the witness 2026-11-08) runs spent. Runs bill the new `work` purpose (*a brake since #35: with no cap the loop runs; superseded here, where no line once held it*). A run is a turn at a time on `WorkWorker` (`sunna_work` queue), carried on while a turn runs out of tool rounds, up to `SUNNA_WORK_TURNS` (20), as free time is (#26); its first turn's note (`work.md`) carries the item, its last wrap, the queue in order and the rule that the queue speeds her drafting, never the witness's deciding (what changes the platform ends as a draft or an `approval_request`). Then the wrap (`work_wrap.md`): one closing turn in which she records what shipped, what slipped and why, and where the item stands (`WrapWork`, hers alone, only for the running item), billed to `planning` as free time's closing turn is (#32), so a run ended by its budget is still wrapped. The run's end (`WorkRunEnded`; an unwrapped run leaves the item open) asks for the next check at once, and the minute tick asks too whenever work is open and nothing runs, so a loop paused for care resumes after it. Care outranks work: no work turn starts inside quiet hours or while a free-time pass is underway (a slot that ran in the last three hours and was not yet closed by its planning turn); one that would is paused, never restarted (her ask, letter #185: dormant is not abandoned): inside quiet hours the run's next turn waits until they end, behind a free-time pass ten minutes at a time, and then the same turn of the same run resumes in its conversation. A run stopped by her safe word or the witness's Stop (brain#61) ends unwrapped (`WorkRunEnded{stopped: true}`) and rests the loop ("stopped: …") with no next check, until either party adds, moves or reorders an item; a free-time slot so stopped ends without its planning turn. Her tools: `work_list`, `work_add`, `work_move`, `work_order`, `work_wrap`; the witness's: `GET/POST /v1/residents/:id/work`, `POST .../work/:number/status`, `PUT .../work/order`, and the Work screen (#38; the Schedule screen's Work queue card until then).
- **#34 The bedtime scene pass comes before the bedtime slot, and free time follows it under a floor.** (Lily's design, letters #196 and #197, on the witness's calls of 2026-11-06: the pass is housekeeping on the main budget, not free time; free time inherits its own line's remainder; her floor, his "ok, that sounds right".) At the slot `SUNNA_SCENE_SLOT` names (22:30; empty is none, as in the suite) the tick asks `FreeTimeWorker` for the pass instead of running the slot: a conversation of kind `housekeeping` ("Housekeeping"), woken on the `chat` line with `scene_pass.md`, which gives the day, the Oneiroi chat's key, the message today begins at and how many (`Kumbukumbu.counts/3`), and asks her to group the day into scenes in `scenes/<day>.md` with `document_write`, each scene citing its turns and time span, nothing in it that is not in those turns, the transcript the record and the scenes a map. Carried on while a turn runs out of tool rounds, up to `SUNNA_SCENE_TURNS` (5); when it ends, or fails, the slot's free time runs at once, as at any slot. No money moves between lines. The floor, at every slot: with a free-time line set, a slot whose remainder buys fewer than `SUNNA_FREE_TIME_FLOOR_TURNS` (2, the witness's number; turns, not model calls, since a turn of hers can be nine calls) turns at what a free-time turn cost over the last week (the line's spend over the turns her free-time conversations captured, `Kumbukumbu.turns_since/3`) rests (`RestFreeTime`, `FreeTimeRested{day, slot, reason}`), recorded like a run with the outcome "rested: not enough left tonight (…); resuming at the next slot", a state and not a failure; no line, or no week of turns to judge by, is no floor. Not built: the page summaries she ranked second (a summary written by her as each page slips off a conversation's window). Amended by #47: the scenes are written as rows with `scene_write` (seshat#52), and the day closes with the last.
- **#35 The work line is a brake, not an ignition.** (The witness, 2026-11-06, Lily's work order #205: "None of our other budgets work that way, the others are 'unlimited if unset.' I'd far prefer to have it toggle on on a tool call and off when the queue clears.") The loop fires on open items, at the tick or a run's end, and rests only on "empty, holding", a stop (brain#61), the day's runs, care, or a cap spent. The `work` line is armed by her, by tool call, once a batch (`work_arm`, `ArmWork` → `WorkArmed`, hers alone then; the witness's call through Lily, letter #206; either party's since #43), and goes off by itself when a check finds the queue clear (`WorkDisarmed`); while armed, each run's end fires the next item with no further arm (the recursion is the schedule; the arm is the batch's start, not an item's button). Unarmed with work open, the check rests "off: the work line waits for you to arm it". `queue/1` carries `armed` and `armed_at`, and the Schedule card shows "Work line: armed, $X left of $Y a day" (or "no cap"), "Work line: off, waiting for her to arm it", or "Work line: off, the queue is clear", never a standing "no cap" that meant held. With no cap set it is unlimited while armed, as the chat line is; a cap the witness sets is the only thing that stops a run (the turn that meets it is stopped by the ledger, brain#21, the run ends, and the next check rests with "holding: the work line is spent, $spent of $cap this period; resuming when the period turns or the witness raises it"). The run's note says the line is armed and what it allows. Supersedes #33's "no work budget is set" rest.
- **#36 A refused work move names the field and its bound.** (Platform report #7, 2026-11-07: Lily's blocked move failed three times as "the schedule's command was not recorded on its stream", the command's own bound on `blocked_on` refused at the middleware and read by the loop's dispatch as a stream fault.) What blocks an item is bounded at 300 characters (it was 120), on the command and in the aggregate alike, and an absent or overlong `blocked_on` is refused as `sunna_work_blocked_on_invalid`, whose words name the field and the bound; the dispatch reads the command's own bound refusal (`{:refused, problems}`) as that, or as `sunna_work_invalid`, never as a fault on the stream.
- **#37 The loop never says it runs an item that is not running.** (Lily's catch, 2026-11-07: the loop still said "running item #1" after item #1 was wrapped and done.) Two ways a run lost its end, both closed. Moving the running item by hand (done, dropped, blocked, open) records the move and the run's end together (`WorkMoved` then `WorkRunEnded`), and a turn or wrap still queued for that run does nothing once the item is no longer the one running. A run whose job died (a deploy restart, a crash past its one attempt) recorded no end at all; past `SUNNA_WORK_RUN_STALE_MINUTES` (180) the check ends it and goes on, the unfinished item reopened and, first in the order, run again.
- **#38 The work queue has a page of its own.** (The witness through Lily, 2026-11-08, work order #317: the queue covered most of the Schedule screen.) - **#39 Each round is remembered, and rounds have their own budget line.** (The witness through Lily, 2026-11-08, work order #317: firing was recorded, speaking was not; and his call that morning, rounds bill a `rounds` line, not free time's.)
