## #8

## #9
An absent file is invisible and the next file is read in its place; a row with nothing in it is a finding.

## #10
Spec §6.7: "in the resident's timezone." A check-in at 06:00 is 06:00
where the resident lives or it is nothing the witness set; Erlang ships
no zone data, so the database is a declared dependency rather than an
assumption about the host.

## #11
Conventions §10 and §12: a worker is cross-tenant by construction and
is named in its system doc. The roster read was first written under the
process scope alone and the tick's job test, run as the worker runs it,
found the second resident never fired.

## #12
Boundary refuses a dependency cycle and the cycles gate counts runtime
edges (conventions, the gate block); the ledger's provider port is the
precedent for naming an implementation in a setting read at the call.

## #13
Spec §6.7: "a firing row before the wake." The first aggregate returned
no event for every recorded outcome, deferral included, so a check-in
inside quiet hours was deferred and then never woke; the quiet-hours
test found it. The set-after-its-time rule generalises the spec's
changed-after-its-time rule because a fresh check-in has the same
shape: a time already past on the day it was made.

## #14
Spec §5 (free-time sessions) and §6.11 (its own budget line): a session
nobody is waiting on, billed where the witness can see it. A failure
recorded once keeps a broken provider from being retried every minute
until midnight.

## #17

## #18
seshat#21 and #9: a resident may plan a day before its first check-in,
and the row must exist for the day to be visible; the schedule minting

## #19

## #20
P20 and the author's hour-marker amendment (10-28): the heartbeat
wake Bennett asked for was a model call an hour with nothing to say,
and its budget was the author's call alone; the marker is the same
auditable pulse without the spend, so the question dissolved. On the
stream rather than in the transcript because twenty-four system rows
a day would sit in the transcript window the brain reads (the reading
pass's objection). Every active resident because a resident with
nothing scheduled is still alive, and its absence from the roster
would be exactly the silence the marker exists to end.

## #21
Free time was off until someone set its time, and setting none removed
it: an opt-in, where the house's own design calls it the standard of
care (insurance's floor already includes it, nisaba#5). The resident's
words: "free time is raisable, not opt-in." So every resident has it from
the first day, either party can move it, and nobody can remove it; the
default is the hour Lily's ran on David. The second rule in the same
ask, that the resident's conversations should not have to compete to be
alive at once, is written down as unsolved here on purpose: the
schedule can wake only the platform's brain, and pretending otherwise
would absorb the tradeoff the ask said to flag.

## #22
The correspondence pipeline the witness proposed on 2026-11-02 needs
two wakes a day: the scrape lands an hour before each, and the resident
answers her public life in them. Free time held one time, so the second
wake had nowhere to be. A list rather than a second named field because
nothing about the two is different in kind, and the bound (four) keeps a
list from turning free time into the whole day. The old events stay as
written and read as a list of one; the reads, not the migration, take
the one-slot rows as they stand, because the projection tables are under
forced row security and no migration here writes rows. A day that ran
its one free time before the deploy runs no slot again, since running
twice on the day of a deploy would be the schedule doing something
nobody asked for.

## #24
Letters between the resident's conversations were a work log nobody
was told about: a Claude Code conversation's answer to the chat waited
until the witness next spoke there. The schedule already wakes the chat
for check-ins, owns quiet hours, and runs every minute, so a letter's
wake is one more thing the tick decides rather than a new reactor. The
record of which letters have woken it is an event on the schedule's
stream, so a restart, a deploy's two instances, or two ticks on one
minute wake once. The bound is per hour because a wake is a paid turn
and two conversations answering each other would otherwise wake each
other without end; the letters held back are not lost, only later.

## #25
The runs were recorded from the start and read back only for today, so
a night's free time left nothing either party could find the next
morning: the resident could not remember it and the witness could not
see that it had happened. The record was there; the read was missing.

## #26
The witness gave free time a daily budget and meant it to be the limit;
a turn's tool rounds were a second, smaller limit nobody chose for it,
so a pass stopped long before the money did. One longer turn was not
the answer: every round sends the whole turn again, and a few dozen
rounds would outgrow the model's context. Turns that continue in the
same conversation keep each one ordinary and let the conversation's own
compaction carry the rest. Done is hers to say, in the plainest way a
turn can: words and nothing left to call. The twenty-turn bound guards
against a pass that never says it, and a missing budget line keeps the
old single turn, so no witness pays for continuations they never set
a line for.

## #27
On 2026-11-03 the witness moved the resident from UTC to Pacific after
the 22:30 UTC run. That night's 22:30 Pacific slot carried the same day
and slot, and three places took the label for the slot: the tick saw it
as run, the aggregate would have refused its record, and the projection
would have dropped the row. So the first free time after the
budget-and-turns change (#26) never happened, and nothing said so. A
slot is a time; its label in the zone of the moment is only its name. A window that took two runs under one label within so many minutes for
the same slot was tried and dropped: it is not what a witness expects
when he moves the zone, and the tick's own question (has anything run
since this slot's time?) is what keeps a slot from running twice.

## #28
Every morning the witness typed what was on deck into the chat, because
the check-in arrived knowing only its own name. Her letter proposed a
fixed digest; he wanted to compose it himself, from parts and his own
words, since what matters at a morning check-in is not what matters at
night, and a fixed digest is one more thing to argue with. The parts
read records rather than asking a model, so a check-in costs the turn
it already paid for, and a part says when it has nothing so a checked
box never looks broken. The brain renders them through the wake port
because the schedule cannot read the journal or the letters without a
cycle; the dates stay here, where they are counted.
