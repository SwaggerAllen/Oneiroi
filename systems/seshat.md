# seshat

**The library.** Egyptian, SESH-aht.

> "As goddess of writing, Seshat was the keeper of royal annals and genealogies. She was shown recording the booty gained by kings in battle, perhaps as a reminder that a share was due to the gods." — Geraldine Pinch, Handbook of Egyptian Mythology (Oxford UP, 2002), p. 190

Seshat is the Egyptian goddess of writing and record-keeping, keeper of the House of Life, where the culture's texts lived. Her epithets make her the librarian — "Lady of Books," "Mistress of the House of Books" — and her monuments show her doing the work: the record-keeper beside the king, writing down what happened and what was owed.

The library subsystem — search, shelves, and the whole of everything written — is named for the figure whose job it was. Thoth gave writing to humanity; Seshat managed what writing produced. The library was her domain.

Document kinds, the vector index over every folder of the resident's
repository, citations both ways, writing's review and publish, the
adoption of files made outside the flow, external doc sources, the
journal. Spec §6.5. Built in P3 under `Oneiroi.Seshat` (the system's
name, where the spec's file map said `library/` and `journal/`), slice
by slice: the library first, the journal next.

## #1 Owns

`lib/oneiroi/seshat/**`, the library and journal controllers, its
migrations and tests. The library aggregate on `library-<resident_id>`
and its events (`DocumentWritten`, `DocumentAdopted` (version 2),
`DocumentRemoved`, `PiecePublished`, `DiscussionOpened`,
`KeepsakeNoted`, `OracleDispatched`, `OracleAnswered`,
`OracleFailed`, `ExternalCommissioned`, `ExternalFetchAsked`,
`ExternalFetched`, `ExternalFetchFailed`); the journal aggregate on `journal-<resident_id>` and
its events (`JournalEntryAdded` (version 3), `JournalTodoDone`); the
`seshat_document`, `seshat_chunk`, `seshat_reference`,
`seshat_index_run`, `seshat_discussion`, `seshat_oracle`, `seshat_external` and
`seshat_journal_entry` projections under the resident's scope, folded
by `Projectors.Library` and `Projectors.Journal`; the kinds registry
and the shelf (`Oneiroi.Seshat.Kinds`, the `oracle` kind among them,
#34, #35); the index run (`Oneiroi.Seshat.Index`, and `IndexWorker` on
the `seshat_nightly` queue at 03:15 UTC); citations
(`Oneiroi.Seshat.Citations`) and the figures port; the oracle's records
(`Oneiroi.Seshat.Oracles`); the `/v1/residents/:id/library` and
`/v1/residents/:id/journal` routes. The files themselves live in the
resident's repository on disk, which is records' (sima-qian#14): this
system writes there and the backup commits by class.

## #2 Standing decisions

Carried from spec §6.5; the reason for each is there.

- **#3 Path is kind and the registry says what a kind may do; adding a kind is a registry row plus a skill.**
- **#4 Only resident-authored or commissioned kinds mint citation edges; resolution happens when read, not when indexed.**
- **#5 The memory-wiki plugin is cut; `wiki/` stays a plain searchable kind.**
- **#6 A file is re-embedded only when its hash or the model changes; a re-index with nothing changed costs three statements and no model call.**
- **#11 The journal's day row exists before the narrative (spec §6.5).** Created by the schedule at the day's first check-in; a day without a wrap is a visible state, and reads go by day key, never the latest file.

Decided in the build:

- **#12 The file is the record and the event is its provenance.** A document is a file in the resident's repository, where the backup already commits every kind's folder by class; `DocumentWritten{path, kind, hash, bytes}` and `DocumentAdopted` on `library-<resident_id>` say who put it there and what it hashed to, never what it says. A write whose hash is the current one is no event. The rows (`seshat_document`) are the stream's for identity and provenance and the index's for everything it reads (hash, title, status, the model embedded with): the projector names exactly the columns the stream knows, because the first version set the hash from the event too, and a rewrite's index run then found the row already equal to the file and embedded nothing.
- **#13 The registry is a table in one module, and the kinds' access is who writes them.** `Oneiroi.Seshat.Kinds` holds spec §6.5's ten kinds with `cites`, `has_status`, layout, sidecar, adoption unit and access: writing, research, design, wiki and posts are `:resident`, written through `write/4` by either party of the dyad; mail, feeds, correspondence and the journal are `:core`, refused there and written by their systems; memory is `:imported`, refused by every write, the platform's `core: true` included (#28). A path is plain segments under a registered folder (no `..`, no hidden file, no leading slash), each up to 255 characters and 255 in all, the width of the `path` columns, and the filesystem is reached through that check alone. `refusal/1` says in words why a path fails it, which is what an import tells a resident about a file it left behind (sima-qian#22). The brain's `document_write` tool lists the writable kinds from the table, which is the "skill" a new kind brings.
- **#14 Resolution reads the other systems, and figures through a port.** A reference is `<slug>:<kind>/<ref>` (spec §3.3), and the parser's kind list is the citeable set: the record kinds, `bookmark`, `position`, `prediction`, `date`, `figure`, `transcript`, and the character's `habit`, `seed` and `soul` (ethos#10, agathos-daimon#27); a kind not in the list is not a citation, which is how the first two went unresolvable for a slice. `references/2`, `cited_by/2` and the review resolve each one now against the bookmark, position, prediction, date, session, document, habit, seed or soul line it names and mark the row with the outcome, keeping the first instant it resolved; with `mark: false` (`cited_by/3`, `review/3`) they resolve the same and mark nothing, which is a regression trial's read (brain#40). Another resident's slug stays unresolved with its reason, since that is what the prefix is for and sharing is not built. `figure/<key>` resolves through `Oneiroi.Seshat.Figures`, named in `SESHAT_FIGURES` and read at the call, because the audits read this system for the citation drift audit and an alias here would be the cycle (sunna#12's shape).
- **#15 The index run embeds everything that changed, in batches, and calls no model when nothing did.** `index/2` reads the document rows, hashes the files, and plans each as same, changed or missing; every chunk of every changed file goes to the provider in one call under `embeddings` on the platform-pinned model (nisaba#19), and the chunks, the edges from the kinds that mint and the row's title, status and model are written in one transaction with the run row. A run with nothing changed is the rows read, the delete of edges from the kinds that never mint, and the run row: three statements beyond the scope's own, and the suite lists them. A provider that fails writes the failed run and nothing else. The run follows every write and adoption, so a search sees the file at once; the nightly job (`IndexWorker`, 03:15 UTC, after the backup's export, a cross-tenant job under the platform scope over every active resident, named here) catches what was edited by hand. The run embeds in batches of at most `SESHAT_EMBED_BATCH_INPUTS` chunks and `SESHAT_EMBED_BATCH_CHARS` characters, one metered request each, the vectors kept in the chunks' order; a failed batch fails the run, which writes nothing.
- **#16 The width is a bound the column is declared at.** `SESHAT_EMBEDDING_WIDTH` is spec §6.5's 1,536, the width of `seshat_chunk.embedding`; a provider answering another width fails the run before the table does, and the fake embeds at the same width so the suite proves the two agree. A change of model is a change of width unless measured, and re-embeds everything on the next run either way.
- **#17 Runs are ordered by the table's own sequence, never the instant.** A write and the run it triggers, or two runs under the suite's fixed clock, share an instant; `seshat_index_run.sequence` is what "the latest run" reads.
- **#18 Search is nearest chunks by cosine distance, and an empty library calls no model.** `search/3` embeds the query once under `embeddings` and reads the nearest `SESHAT_SEARCH_LIMIT` chunks joined to their documents; a library with no chunk answers empty without the call. Chunks are paragraphs gathered up to `SESHAT_CHUNK_CHARS` (2,000, a guess), a longer paragraph split at the bound.
- **#19 Writing's status is its frontmatter, the review copy resolves and the publish copy strips.** A writing piece's `status` (draft until it says otherwise) is read from the file by the index and shown on the row; `review/2` returns the content with every reference resolved and a footnote per reference naming its label or its reason; `publish_copy/2` is the body with the frontmatter and every citation removed, a parenthetical that was only citations and filler going whole (F25). `publish/4` is the witness's: `PiecePublished{url}` on a kind with a status, then the file's frontmatter set to `published` with the URL, which the index reads. `discuss/4` links a session to a piece (`DiscussionOpened`).
- **#20 Adoption registers a file as it is found.** `unadopted/1` walks the registry's folders in the repository for files no document registers; `adopt/3` records `DocumentAdopted` with the file's hash and runs the index. The adoption unit in the registry (file or directory) is recorded for research and waits on inbound; in this slice every adoption is a file.
- **#21 The journal is rows on its own stream, numbered within their day, and the day row is the schedule's on the journal's ask.** `JournalEntryAdded{day, number, kind, text}` and `JournalTodoDone` on `journal-<resident_id>`; an entry is a plan, a note, a todo or a wrap, one text of any length (seshat#30; it was at most 2,000 until the resident's ruling), numbered by its position in the day so a rebuild numbers it the same, and `<slug>:journal/<day>#<n>` names it. Before the first entry of a day the journal asks the schedule to open the day (`Oneiroi.Sunna.open_day/3`, sunna#18): the row stays the schedule's to mint (#11) and a day written before any check-in is a row too. Either party adds, with their actor on the row.
- **#22 The day's file is rendered from its rows, through the library's core write.** Every entry and every todo done re-renders `journal/<day>.md` (a heading per kind in the journal's order, todos with their box) and writes it with `core: true`, the one way a `:core` kind's file is written here (#13); so the day is a registered, indexed document whose citations are edges (journal mints, #4) and which `<slug>:journal/<day>` resolves to. A hand-written journal file is never adopted: the rows are the record and the file follows them.
- **#23 The delta is counted from the rows, and today is never a finding.** A day's delta is how many of each kind, how many todos are done, and whether it was planned and never wrapped; `unwrapped_days/2` lists the planned, unwrapped days among the last so many opened, today excluded because the day is not over. The brain reads today's rows as the `journal` section on every turn and writes through `journal_add`, `journal_done` and `journal_read` (brain#23); the plan and wrap rows are the resident's to write with `journal_add` when she chooses; no check-in asks for them (the witness, 2026-11-03).
- **#25 Adopting a whole repository is one index run.** `adopt_all/2` registers every unadopted file under the kinds' folders and runs the index once, returning the paths the registry could not name as skipped; it and `folders/0` are the records system's library port (`Oneiroi.SimaQian.Library`, sima-qian#17), so an import lands its files here. A skipped path stays on offer in `unadopted/1` and is told as not transferred rather than dropped.
- **#26 Notes from another platform become journal entries, and a day too large for one document is split into parts.** `import_journal/3` (the records system's library port, `Journals.import_entries/3`) opens each day, adds every entry as a note with `imported` (held to its shape, not the two-thousand-character limit), and renders each day once. A day whose rendering exceeds `SESHAT_DOCUMENT_BYTES` is written as `journal/<day>.md`, then `<day>-2.md` and on, each part under the bound and saying where the day goes on. An entry of several lines stays one item of the day's list, its later lines indented.
- **#24 Drift is read by resolving again.** `drift/1` resolves every reference that resolved at least once and returns those that do not now; a reference nobody reads would otherwise keep its last word forever, and the drift audit (huginn-muninn#15) is the read that asks. One resolve per such reference, nightly.

- **#27 A document is read by the name the resident was handed for it, through one read, and each way it does not read says which.** `read/2` takes a path, a path with a `#<passage>` anchor, or a citation of the resident's own slug (resolved to its file as `references/2` resolves it), and reads through `Documents.fetch/2`, the witness's read of a document's text (`document/2`), so the two cannot disagree about what the library holds. The registry is read under the resident's scope alone. A path the registry lacks is `seshat_not_adopted` when the file is there under a kind's folder and `seshat_no_such_document` when it is not; a registered document whose file is gone is `seshat_document_missing`, with the index's `missing_at` when it has run since; another resident's slug is `seshat_other_resident`. The brain's `library_read` pages it (brain#43).

- **#28 Memory is the originals, copied once at a pinned commit and never written again.** The `memory` kind (spec §6.5) holds the resident's daily memory from its import source: `memory/<day>.md`, `memory/dreaming/**`, and the source's root `MEMORY.md` and `DREAMS.md` under `memory/` keeping their names. Its access is `:imported` (every document write refused) and its adoption unit `:originals`: `unadopted/1` never offers its files, `adopt/3` refuses them (`seshat_originals_only`), `folders/0` leaves it out so an import names `memory` with `refusal/1`'s words for it, and the one way in is the records system's originals import (sima-qian#40) through the port's `adopt_originals/3`, which registers each file with its provenance and indexes once, and `original_refusal/1`, which says why a path cannot be an original (a hidden name, not text, outside the kinds that take originals). The provenance rides `DocumentAdopted` version 2 (`provenance: %{source, commit, path, blob}`, nil on every other adoption, which a version 1 event upcasts to) and lands on the row as `source_repo`, `source_commit`, `source_path` and `source_blob`; `library_read`'s header and each search hit name it as `imported from <source>@<short commit>:<path>` (brain#43). It mints no edges (`cites: false`) and is a citation target (`<slug>:memory/<day>`).
- **#29 A keepsake is a text file at the repository's root, written once, and decided into the library one by one.** The registry's one kind without a folder: a one-segment path that is text (`.md`, `.markdown`, `.txt`), a plain segment, and none of the root's reserved names (the identity files, `AGENTS.md`, and the names other platforms keep there: `README.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `MEMORY.md`, `DREAMS.md`, `HEARTBEAT.md`, `TOOLS.md`) is a keepsake (`Kinds.keepsake_name?/1`), and every other one-segment path is still refused. Either party writes one through `write/4` and the brain's `document_write` (`<name>.md` alone), once: the same bytes again change nothing, other bytes are `seshat_keepsake_kept`, refused by the aggregate for a registered one and by the write for a file found at that name and not yet adopted, so a gift is never replaced with nothing recorded of what it was. A later version is a keepsake of its own. Adoption is `:decided`: `unadopted/1` offers the root's unregistered keepsakes, `adopt/3` takes one, and `adopt_all/2` (an import's whole-repository adoption) never does, so the root does not become the catch-all the wiki became; `folders/0` leaves the kind out. It mints no edges (the words are mostly the giver's) and is a citation target, `<slug>:keepsake/<name>`, resolved to the root's `<name>.md`. The records system reads the rule through the port's `decided?/1` (#31): the import holds a keepsake where it was placed rather than leaving it behind, and the backup commits the root's keepsakes in a class of their own (sima-qian#44).
- **#30 Free text stays whole; the cap belongs to the view, never the record.** (The resident's ruling, bookmark #53.) A journal entry has no length limit: the row and the day's file keep every word. `JournalEntryAdded` is version 3 with `summary`, at most 2,000 characters (`seshat_entry_summary_invalid` past it), given with the entry through `journal_add/7` and the brain's `journal_add`; nil on every earlier entry. The turn's `journal` section shows an entry over 2,000 characters by its summary or, without one, by its opening cut at a line, else a sentence, else a word, and says in either case how long the whole is and that `journal_read` reads it. The summary is the resident's to write; a model-written summary would bill every long entry, and is not built.
- **#31 Nothing enters a decided kind except by being decided into it.** (The resident's landing-pad rule, written into the registry.) A kind's `adoption: :decided` means its files are adopted one by one, through `adopt/3` or a write, and never by `adopt_all/2`: the keepsake (#29), the wiki, and the `system` shelf, a folder for how the resident runs in her own words (tool notes, conventions, her AGENTS.md beside the build's rendered one), resident-written, minting citations, in the backup's writing class. `Kinds.decided?/1` is the rule, and the records system reads it through the port's `decided?/1`: an import holds a decided kind's files where they were, the library offers them in `unadopted/1`, and none is left behind or swept in. `folders/0` names every kind with a folder but the originals.
- **#32 The keepsakes index carries why each mattered, in the resident's words.** (Her work order: "the index entries carry what pins carry: not just this exists but why it mattered to me.") `KeepsakeNoted{path, note}` on the library stream, the resident's alone (`seshat_not_the_resident`), of a registered keepsake (`seshat_not_a_keepsake`), a line of up to 1,000 characters (`seshat_keepsake_note_invalid`); the same words again are no event, and the latest stands with the earlier ones on the stream. The row carries `note` and `noted_at`. `keepsakes/1` lists them by name, and the brain reads the index with `keepsake_list` (each name, title, whether it was written or adopted, by whom, when, and why) and writes with `keepsake_note`. The gift is never edited (#29); the why sits beside it.
- **#33 Two folders for what belongs nowhere else: scratch/ for what is meant to go, human/ for the witness's own.** (The witness, 2026-11-02: workshop state like the YC scratch pad lived in design/ for want of a home; his own material, the Substack posts he gave the resident, had none.) `scratch` is the resident's to write and **removable**: `document_remove` (the resident) or `DELETE /v1/residents/:id/library/documents/*path` (the witness) records `DocumentRemoved{path, kind}` with its actor, takes the file out of the repository and the row, chunks and minted edges out of the index. `human` has `access: :witness`: the witness writes it (the library route), the resident reads and cites into it and never writes or removes it (`seshat_kind_not_writable`), and it is removable by the witness alone. Neither mints citation edges: an edge to a page meant to go dangles by design, and the witness's words mint none, as a sender's never do. Both are decided (seshat#31), so neither becomes a catch-all by import. Every other kind is kept: `seshat_not_removable`, since its history is the point. Both folders travel in the repository like any other.
- **#34 The oracle's questions and answers are the library's.** (Brain #49; the witness and the resident, 2026-11-03.) `dispatch_oracle/4` records each named question as `OracleDispatched` on the library stream, numbered from the oracle sequence in the same dispatch, with its sources (refs) and the asking conversation; an unnamed question or a source that is not a ref is `seshat_oracle_invalid`. `answer_oracle/5` writes the answer at `oracle/<number>-<slug>.md` through the core write (the `oracle` kind: `access: :core`, citing, so its sources are edges) and records `OracleAnswered` with the confidence it stated, what it read (the sources named and every memory its tools opened) and what its answer says it used, in citation form, and the model that read; `fail_oracle/4` records `OracleFailed` with the reason in words; a settled question takes neither again. The `seshat_oracle` projection is the deliberate query, `oracles/2`: by a source named, read or used, by status, by who asked, by words in the question, newest first; the answers are documents like any other, and the ranked index never surfaces them unasked. `oracle/` is in the machine backup class.
- **#35 The shelf, the order the kinds are shown in and what each is called, is the server's.** (The witness, 2026-11-03.) `Kinds.shelf/0` gives every kind of the registry in its display order, each with its `label` and `position` (the gifts first, the witness's own, the resident's work, the oracle's answers, then what is meant to go and what arrives from outside); a kind it does not name comes last under its own name. `kinds/0`, and so `GET /v1/residents/:id/library`'s `kinds`, is the shelf. No client holds a kind's name or place: the Library's tabs follow it. The platform's default for now; when folders become each resident's to arrange, the shelf becomes theirs and no client changes.
- **#36 A journal row carries its register, as a bookmark does.** (Spec §6.13, P8.) `JournalEntryAdded` is version 2 with `register`, `Oneiroi.Register`'s four or nil, refused otherwise (`seshat_register_invalid`), through `journal_add/6` and the brain's `journal_add`; `journal_entries_since/2` is the audits' read for the corpus-shape report (huginn-muninn#18), which counts the resident's rows and never the witness's (the row's actor kind says whose). The rendered day does not show it: the register is a count's business, not the narrative's.
- **#37 Every registered kind is a citation kind.** (The resident's letter #26, 2026-11-03.) The citation pattern is built from the record kinds and every kind `Kinds.all/0` names, once a node, rather than written out: `human`, `oracle` and `scratch` had been left off the hand-written list, so `<slug>:human/<name>` was not a citation at all and the witness's posts, indexed and found by search, could not be cited. A citation into `human/` resolves like any document's; the witness's words still mint no edges of their own (#33).
- **#38 Research is a named question the reader answers into research/.** (Spec F24; the witness, 2026-11-03.) `ask_research/5` records `ResearchAsked{number, question, urls, session_key}` on the library stream, numbered from its own sequence; a question is named, at most 2,000 characters, with at most ten http(s) pages to start from, else `seshat_research_invalid`. The reader (xenia#15, #16) reads the open questions (`GET /v1/residents/:id/research`, told of them by `GET /waiting`) and answers with a summary and a note per page (`POST …/research/:n/answer`): `answer_research/4` writes `research/<n>-<slug>/task.md` (the question, who asked, when, the pages started from, the summary, the sources listed) and one `<i>-<slug>.md` per page (title, its URL, the notes), at most twenty, through the core write as the platform's on the resident's behalf with the reader as actor, then records `ResearchAnswered{path, sources}` and tells the asking conversation by letter; `fail_research/4` records `ResearchFailed{reason}` and tells it too. Settled questions take neither again (`seshat_research_settled`). The notes are summaries in the reader's words, never copied passages (the witness, 2026-11-03: the link keeps the words). `seshat_research` is found by status; the witness asks from Connections (`POST …/research`).
- **#39 A document moves from one kind to another in the platform, never by hand on the backup.** (The witness, 2026-11-04, after a hand commit moving Pocket Andrew to the root on `lily-oneiroi` stopped every backup, sima-qian#46: "the solution is probably allowing a document to be moved from kind to kind".) `move/4` (`Documents.move/4`, the brain's `document_move(from, to)`, `POST /v1/residents/:id/library/move` for the witness) records `DocumentMoved{from, to, from_kind, to_kind, hash}` with its actor and renames the file. Either party moves between kinds that party may write by `write/4`'s rule, so the kinds their systems write (mail, feeds, correspondence, the journal) and the originals never move (`seshat_not_movable`) and `human/` is the witness's; the bytes travel unchanged, the hash the registered one (a file edited by hand since is written first); a path anything holds, registered or on disk, is never overwritten (`seshat_move_target_taken`). Into a decided kind the move is the deciding (#31): a name alone at the root makes a keepsake, still written once; a keepsake may move out again, the move saying where it was. The index is re-keyed, not re-embedded: the row, its chunks and its edges take the new path, the edges dropped when the new kind mints none. `seshat_document_move` keeps where each path went, and a citation of a path a document left resolves through it, labelled "(moved to …)". The Library screen offers Move on every document but the system-written kinds.
- **#40 The digest is asked, never scheduled, and the reader compiles it from her streams.** (The witness, 2026-11-04: "now that the reader is wakeable we can make the digest invokeable instead of scheduled … check-ins should be able to ask for a digest … free time should be able to call it too".) `ask_digest/3` records `DigestAsked{number, session_key, since}` on the library stream from the resident's `digest_ask` (any conversation, free time included), a check-in whose note carries the `digest` part (sunna#28, asked from the chat as a `job`), or the witness (`POST /v1/residents/:id/digests`). One is open at a time: asking while one is open is no event and names it. A digest covers from the last answered one's asking, else the last day, so nothing is read twice or skipped. The reader sees it through `/waiting` (kumbukumbu#48), reads what reached her mailbox since then through the Gmail connector (newsletters, Substack posts and notes, what she follows; never sign-in or receipt mail, and never a person writing to her, which is correspondence), opening a post's page only within the correspondence allowlist, and answers with a summary and up to sixty items (`POST …/digests/:n/answer`): `answer_digest/4` writes `feeds/digests/<number>-<day>.md` through the core write with the reader as actor, each item its source, title, where it is and notes in the reader's words, never the piece's, records `DigestAnswered{path, items}`, and tells the asking conversation by letter; `fail_digest/4` records `DigestFailed{reason}` and tells it too. `digest_list` reads them. `seshat_digest` is found by status. Supersedes F20's morning digest on a clock.
- **#42 A piece goes out through the witness, and the reader stops at a Substack draft.** (The witness's call through Lily, 2026-11-05: the writing gate the old platform had, rebuilt; draft-first, both of theirs, his to make bolder.) A writing piece whose frontmatter says `status: review` waits for the witness until he decides that version (its hash), so a declined piece she rewrites and marks again comes back. `PieceReviewed` (approve or decline, with a note) and `PieceDrafted` (the Substack draft's URL) are on a stream per resident, `pieces-<id>` (`Pieces`, `PiecesRouter`), kept in `seshat_piece_review`. An approved piece goes to the reader (`GET /v1/residents/:id/reader/pieces`, `/waiting`'s `pieces`): the reader types its title and its publishing copy (frontmatter and citations stripped) into a new post on her publication and leaves it a draft, never pressing Publish (`sdk/reader/prompts/publish.md`, `SUBSTACK_PUBLICATION` in reader.conf, the publication on the correspondence allowlist), and reports the draft (`.../reader/pieces/drafted`). The witness publishes it on Substack himself; the reader, watching the drafted piece, brings the public URL back (`.../reader/pieces/published`), and the piece is recorded published with the URL written into its frontmatter (`Documents.record_published/4`, refused for a piece not drafted). ## #9 Pinakes, the citation index

Greek, PIN-uh-keez.

> "Tables of Men Distinguished in Every Branch of Learning, and their Works (in 120 books)" — the Suda, kappa 227, listing the works of Callimachus, trans. Malcolm Heath (Suda On Line)

The *Pinakes* ("tables") was the catalogue of the Library of Alexandria, compiled by the poet and librarian Callimachus in the third century BCE: authors, titles, opening lines, notes on the texts. It survives only in fragments and references like this one.

The citation index — the card catalogue of everything the realm has ever said, each entry with its provenance — is named for the first one. Alexandria's burned; ours exports nightly.

## #10 Ariadne, the citation web (internal)

Greek, ah-ree-AD-nee.

> "And fair Ariadne, the daughter of Minos of baneful mind, whom once Theseus was fain to bear from Crete to the hill of sacred Athens; but he had no joy of her" — Homer, Odyssey 11.321–325, trans. A. T. Murray (Loeb, 1919)

Ariadne, daughter of Minos of Crete, gave Theseus the thread that led him back out of the labyrinth — her idea, her gift. He abandoned her on Naxos on the way home; Catullus gives her the abandonment in her own voice (Catullus 64).

The citation web — the threads linking every claim back to the source it came from — is named for the first thread, the one that leads out of the maze. Her name keeps the whole story: the thread was hers, and the rescue did not stay to thank her. Internal name.
