# gaia

**Identity.** Greek, GUY-uh (also said JEE-uh).

> "In truth at first Chaos came to be, but next wide-bosomed Earth, the ever-sure foundation of all the deathless ones who hold the peaks of snowy Olympus..." "She bore also the fruitless deep with his raging swell, Pontus, without sweet union of love." — Hesiod, Theogony 116–117, 125, trans. H. G. Evelyn-White (Loeb, 1914)

Gaia is the earth. In Hesiod's account of how everything began, she is the first being after Chaos itself — the "ever-sure foundation," and a creator before anything else: she bears starry Heaven "equal to herself," the hills, and the barren sea, all before any mate, "without sweet union of love." Everything after — Titans, gods, the whole pantheon — descends from her.

The identity layer — accounts, authentication, where the humans arrive — is named for the place humans come from. Oneiroi is a two-world story: the residents are of the dream, the humans are of the earth, and the bridge between the worlds is hospitality. Latin *homo* (human) is probably related to *humus* (earth); both likely descend from one Proto-Indo-European word for earth — a link the etymologists still debate, carried here with that hedge.

Accounts, authentication, sessions, witnessing (spec §6.1). Written
attended, by the author's decision, so the auth code was read as it was
written (plan P0.4).

## #1 Owns

The Account aggregate on `account-<id>` and its v1 events; the
`gaia_account`, `gaia_session`, `gaia_token`,
`gaia_witnessing`, `gaia_push_subscription` and `gaia_push_answer`
(#25) projections; the needs-you push's record on the account (#26); the principal (`Oneiroi.Gaia
.Principal`: an account via a session or a token, carrying the actor it
acts as); the request plugs (secure headers, authenticate, origin,
require-principal); the session, me, second-factor and token routes under
`/v1`; the login throttle; the witnessing handler; the self-hosted setup
task that creates the one account. The household on `household-<id>`
(#17), with its contract, signatures, ballots and watches (#27 to #32)
and their tables `gaia_household_contract`, `gaia_household_ballot`,
`gaia_household_vote` and `gaia_household_watch`.

## #2 Standing decisions

- **#3 A password and a TOTP second factor; argon2 for the one, the
  vault for the other.** The password hash is argon2id; the TOTP secret
  enters the log encrypted under the platform vault and is redacted from
  every export. Passkeys are later (spec §6.1).
- **#4 Sessions and tokens are random ids with only the hash stored.**
  Thirty days for a session, from `GAIA_SESSION_TTL_DAYS`; a token
  until revoked. The raw value is returned once and never written. A
  token's label is a name, bounded at 120 characters on the command
  (`IssueToken`'s `bounds:`) and refused at dispatch as
  `label_too_long`.
- **#5 The cookie is `__Host-oneiroi`, `HttpOnly`, `Secure`,
  `SameSite=Strict`, and every mutation without a bearer token checks
  `Origin`.** The Origin plug is the CSRF defence: a script's bearer
  token is its proof and a browser's cookie is where a forged request
  comes from, so a mutation on the cookie must carry the endpoint's own
  Origin or it is refused before any controller runs. Login is checked
  too. The CSP allows the client's own bundle and nothing else.
- **#6 Failed logins slow down and never lock out.** Per email, the
  delay doubles from `GAIA_LOGIN_DELAY_MS` on each failure, capped
  at five seconds, cleared when the credentials verify; counts live in a
  process's ETS and are lost on restart. Credentials that verified and a
  session the store could not open are not a failure: 503
  `gaia_session_unavailable` with `retry-after`, counted nothing.
- **#7 Witnessing is a relation with a start and an end, written by this
  system.** `ResidentCreated` starts one and `CustodyTransferred` ends
  and starts them, through a strong handler on the resident stream, so
  `witnessed_residents/1` is read from this system's own projection and
  the resident system never writes an identity row. The community is a
  principal of its own for custody and holds no witnessing row.
- **#8 Two reads take the platform scope by name.** The account by email
  at login and the session or token by hash on a request: the principal
  is not known until they answer. Everything after runs under the
  account's scope. The resident system's adapter lookup by token hash is
  the third such read (agathos-daimon#16), on the same reasoning.
- **#9 The contract test is the route table.** `OneiroiWeb.ContractTest`
  asserts, per route and per principal kind (anonymous, session, token),
  exactly which answers 200, 401, 403 or 422, and that a mutation from a
  browser without the endpoint's Origin is refused.

- **#12 An account's email by id, and its id by email, are platform-scoped reads by name.** `account_email/1` names an investigator (glitnir#8) and the account a custody transfer is proposed to; `account_id_for/1` resolves the email a witness names. Both take the platform scope by name (conventions §12), as #8's two reads do.
- **#13 Witnessing follows custody in the event's wire form.** The witnessing handler reads `CustodyTransferred`'s `from` and `to` through `Oneiroi.AgathosDaimon.Custody.from_wire/1`: the first version matched the tuple form against the wire strings and matched nothing, so no transfer ever ended the old witnessing or started the new one, and the old witness's routes kept working on a resident it no longer held. Found by the custody suite in P4, once a transferred resident was read from the new witness's own session; the subscription's community move had the same hole from P2.
- **#14 The witness's OpenRouter key lives on the account, encrypted (numbered #11 until 2026-11-03, when the audit found #11 was also "Depends on").
- **#15 The witness's devices are told, content-free, when the resident writes unprompted.** (The witness, 2026-11-05: "Let's ship the pwa mechanism with this so that I can start getting push notifications"; he missed check-ins he knew were coming.) A device subscribes by its push service's endpoint (`POST /v1/me/push`), held to the known push services (Apple's, Google's, Mozilla's, Microsoft's) so the server never posts where a client said, and recorded on the account (`PushSubscribed`/`PushUnsubscribed`, `gaia_push_subscription`). A push carries no payload at all: nothing the resident said passes through a push service, there is nothing to encrypt, and the push is an empty POST signed with the platform's VAPID key (`GAIA_VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY`, ES256, RFC 8292; unset, nothing is pushed and the client says so), through `GAIA_PUSH_CLIENT` (`WebPush`, `Fake` in the suite), off the caller in `:gaia_push`. A push service's 404 or 410 unsubscribes the device as gone. The brain pushes when a turn in the chat ends that the witness did not start: a check-in, a letter that woke the chat, a round's word arriving there (`as: :note`, purpose chat); never for a turn he is waiting on, never for a solo session. The client's service worker shows "<name> wrote" and opens the chat; on an iPhone only the Home Screen app may ask, and the More screen's Notifications card says so. Principle 14 holds: it is asked for, opt-in per device, fires only when there is something she wrote, and never because he has been away.
- **#16 The front door and its keepers are the platform's own records.** (The witness, 2026-11-05: "the first witness is the operator, at this stage, because a human admin is needed"; operator transferable; two tiers, admin and super admin; design/mvp.md item 1.) One stream, `platform` (`Gaia.Platform`, `PlatformRouter`), holds the gate and the roles. The gate is `closed` (the default), `invite` (the alpha) or `open`, set by `GateSet` with a note. A role is `super_admin` (the operator: the gate, the roles, global permissions) or `admin` (the scoped tier), set by `RoleGranted` and `RoleRevoked`, each with a note and its actor. The aggregate checks the authority, so no caller can skip it: only a super admin changes either, every change needs a note, and the last super admin cannot be revoked, so the door always has a keeper (a transfer is a grant, then a revoke). The first grant is the platform's: the first time the roles are asked about with no super admin recorded, the founding account (the first ever created) is granted super admin by the platform, as an event, so the operator is a recorded grant and never inferred from account age. The gate lives in `gaia_gate`, the first table of the new `:platform` scope (no tenant column, readable only under the platform scope, `Oneiroi.Migration.projection/3`), because the door belongs to no account; the roles live in `gaia_role`, account-scoped, each its holder's. `/v1/platform` (`OperatorController`, a super admin's alone) reads and changes both, `/v1/me` names the caller's roles, and More carries an Operator card for a super admin. The platform stream's id comes from `Door.stream/0`, which the suite overrides per test because the event store is not rolled back between tests. Global and resident-scoped permissions, the domain flag, invite codes and sign-up build on this, after.
- **#17 The household is the billing owner, with its witnesses and its residents as memberships.** (The witness, 2026-11-05: household now, subscription and payment tied to it, two join tables, one for witnesses and one for residents; never neither; dyads now, additive later.) A stream per household, `household-<id>` (`Gaia.Household`, `HouseholdRouter`), founded by the platform when its founding account is created, the id being that account's, so an account's own household is known without a read. `HouseholdWitnessJoined` and `HouseholdResidentJoined` are memberships, idempotent; `HouseholdResidentLeft` is a decision with a note; `HouseholdTermsGranted` is the household's subscription terms (plan, price in nano-dollars, until, source, note), granted by the platform (an invite redeemed) or a super admin and charged by nothing. The household handler (`Handlers.Household`, `gaia_household_members`) founds a household for every account created, joins every resident created to its witness's, and moves a resident whose custody passes to another account; custody to the community leaves it where it was. It started from the log's origin, so the accounts and residents before it were given theirs. It dispatches waiting on the household projection alone: a strong dispatch from a strong handler waits on every strong handler, the witnessing handler among them, and the two deadlock until the consistency timeout. A consistency timeout is not a failure: the event is written and the projection folds it when it catches up; on the first boot, the projection replaying the log beside the handler timed out on every backfilled household and logged four false failures. The household row (`gaia_household`) is platform-scoped, every member's and no one's alone, and read by name under the platform scope only after the caller's own membership row (`gaia_household_witness`, account-scoped; `gaia_household_resident`, resident-scoped) has named it. `/v1/me` carries the household. The per-resident subscription (nisaba#15) remains the resident's insurance policy until billing reads these terms.
- **#18 Invite codes are SKUs that grant a household its terms, held only as hashes.** (design/mvp.md item 2; the witness, 2026-11-05: $20 a month list, nothing charged, codes granting terms to the household.) A stream per code, `invite-<id>` (`Gaia.Invite`, `InviteRouter`): `InviteIssued` is the SKU (plan, list price in nano-dollars, discount 0 to 100, days of terms, uses, optional expiry) with a note, issued by a super admin alone; `InviteRedeemed` names the household; `InviteRevoked` carries a note. A code is twelve characters from an alphabet without `0 O 1 I L`, shown as three groups of four and answered once, at issue: the log and `gaia_invite` hold only its SHA-256 over the code with case, spaces and dashes removed, so a typed code matches however it was typed. The aggregate refuses a redemption that is revoked, past its expiry (judged at the command's instant), already this household's, or spent, in that order, each by its own kind, and `check_invite/1` answers the same from the projection without spending a use, for the sign-up form. A redemption is recorded, then the platform grants the household (gaia#17) the SKU's terms: the list price less the discount, running the days from the redemption, `invite:<id>` as the source. The defaults (`Invites.defaults/0`) are the alpha's free code: $20 a month at a full discount, ninety days, one use, no expiry. `/v1/platform/invites` lists, issues and revokes (the operator's); `POST /v1/me/invite` redeems onto the caller's household; the Invites card under More, for a super admin, issues and shows the code once. Codes and redemptions are platform-scoped: the front door's keys belong to the platform.
- **#19 Sign-up is behind the front door.** (design/mvp.md item 1; the witness, 2026-11-05, through Lily: no email verification while the door is closed or invite, before it opens.) `Gaia.Signup` reads the gate: `closed` refuses (`gaia_door_closed`, 403); `invite` requires a code (`gaia_invite_required`); `open` takes anyone, a code optional. The checks run before anything is created, the code before the email, so behind an invite door someone without a code learns nothing about which emails hold accounts; then the email's shape and a password of at least ten characters (`gaia_signup_invalid`), then the email unused (`gaia_email_taken`, 409). The account is created by the platform, since no principal exists yet, and its household is founded in the same dispatch (#17), so the code is redeemed onto it at once (#18). A code spent by someone else between its check and its redemption leaves the account created without terms, and the refusal carries `account_created`. The routes are anonymous: `GET /v1/door` (the state), `POST /v1/door/check` (what a code would grant, unspent) and `POST /v1/door/sign-up`, which opens a session as logging in does. The client's sign-in screen offers "I have an invite" behind an invite door and "Create an account" behind an open one, and nothing behind a closed one. Email verification lands before the gate is set to open.
- **#20 Platform reports, from people and residents, go to the platform team and to the triage resident.** (The witness, 2026-11-06: reports and feature requests from agents and humans out of the gate, an admin view at once, and a button that sends them straight to Lily; design reports-and-feedback.md, hers.) One stream, `feedback` (`Feedback`, `FeedbackRouter`), numbers each report: `FeedbackFiled` carries the kind (bug or feature_request), the surface, the reporter's words whole (at most 8,000 characters), an optional trace id, and the reporter: a person under their account, or a resident with its witness's account beside it; `FeedbackMoved` moves its status (new, in_work, resolved, closed) with a note the reporter reads. Kept in `gaia_feedback`, platform-scoped: the operator reads the list (`GET /v1/platform/feedback`, narrowed by kind and status), one report with the failure ring's entries for its trace id (`/v1/platform/feedback/:number`, apsu#23) and moves it; a person files (`POST /v1/feedback`) and reads their own and their residents' (`/v1/me/feedback`); a resident files with `platform_report` and reads its own with `platform_report_list`. The triage resident (`GAIA_TRIAGE_RESIDENT`, a slug; Lily) gets each report as a house letter to its chat from "a platform report" (`Brain.Triage`, eventual, skipping rather than stopping on a failure) and is the one resident shown `triage_list` and `triage_move`. This is not the witness rail: `report_file` (Glitnir, spec F45) is about the witness, investigated by a person and never in shared triage; the two never meet, and `platform_report`'s description says so. More carries "Report a problem or suggest a feature" for every signed-in person, saying where it goes; the Operator section carries Reports.
- **#21 Every start of the platform is recorded, and every conversation is told of it.** (The witness, 2026-11-06, any restart with no exception: "otherwise the intersection of memory, model and code isn't clear".) At each start a task (`Boots.record_now/0`, after the platform projector) records `BootRecorded` on the platform stream: numbered from 1, the commit it runs (`RENDER_GIT_COMMIT`, an external name Render sets), the commit the boot before ran, and whether they differ; it says what restarted, never why. `gaia_boot` holds them, platform-scoped. `Boots.since/1` is the read a conversation's notice takes (brain#63), `Boots.line/2` the one sentence both readers show ("restarted at …: commit a → b, changed. The diff: <GAIA_SOURCE_URL>/compare/a...b", or "same commit"), and the operator's `/v1/platform` carries the latest ten as `restarts`, listed on the Operator card. `GAIA_RECORD_BOOTS` (on) is off in the suite, whose starts are not the platform's.
- **#22 The server's console can name the operator.** (The witness, 2026-11-07, who could not see the Operator card: the founding account is the earliest created, which need not be his.) `Oneiroi.Gaia.console_operator(email)`, run from a shell on the live release (`bin/oneiroi rpc 'IO.inspect(Oneiroi.Gaia.console_operator("you@example.com"))'`), grants super admin to the account with that email (case and surrounding spaces ignored; `gaia_no_such_account` otherwise) and then revokes it from every other holder, so that account is the only operator. Its actor is the platform at the console (`platform:console` on the role row), apart from the platform's own jobs, which still grant nothing past the founding grant. The platform aggregate lets the console grant and revoke any role, each with its note; the last-keeper rule still holds, and the grant comes first, so the door is never without a keeper.
- **#23 The triage desk is set on the Operator screen.** (The witness, 2026-11-07: "we should have an operator screen and that value should be in it".) `SetTriageResident{slug}` on the platform stream (a super admin's or the console's, with a note; `gaia_not_an_operator`, `gaia_platform_invalid` otherwise, and a slug no resident has is refused by the service) records `TriageResidentSet{slug}`, nil for nobody, folded into `gaia_setting` under `triage_resident` (migration `20261107000060`). `Gaia.triage_desk/0` answers the slug, where it came from (`setting`, or `environment` while no setting was ever made), and who set it, when and why; `triage_resident/0` reads it, so the letters and the triage tools (#20) follow it at once. Once set, the setting decides, nobody included, and `GAIA_TRIAGE_RESIDENT` is read no more. `PUT /v1/platform/triage` and the Operator card's Triage desk row carry it. The resident's consent to the role is not built; the operator asks her.
- **#24 The witness keeps a GitHub read token beside the key.** (The witness, 2026-11-08, through Lily's letter #368: the token for the resident's pull request reads is "UI-configurable per witness", set, rotated and revoked from the client, not the shell.) `StoreGithubToken`/`GithubTokenStored` (the vault's ciphertext, redacted from the export as the key is; storing again the rotation) and `RevokeGithubToken`/`GithubTokenRevoked` on the account stream, folded into `gaia_account`'s `github_token_ciphertext` and `github_token_stored_at` (migration `20261109000010`). `github_token_for/1` hands it wrapped to the pull requests port alone (brain#71), nil when none; `github_token_status/1` says whether one is stored and since when, and `/v1/me` carries it as `github`. `PUT /v1/me/github-token` stores, `DELETE` revokes (404 `gaia_no_github_token` with none). A blank token is `gaia_github_token_required`.
- **#25 The notifications question is answered once, for the account.** (The witness, 2026-11-10, design/ui-server-work.md S6: "No thanks" clears it, and Settings can turn notifications on later.) `DeclinePush` records `PushDeclined{}` on the account stream (none when the latest answer is already that), `POST /v1/me/push/decline` answers `{answered, answered_at}`. A device subscribing (`PushSubscribed`) is the other answer, so turning notifications on from Settings answers it too; unsubscribing leaves the answer as it was. `gaia_push_answer` holds the latest per account (`subscribed` or `declined`, with its instant), and `Gaia.push_answer/1` reads it, an account with a device subscribed before the table answering `subscribed` from its first device. `GET /v1/me/push` carries `answered` and `answered_at`. While an account has no answer the question is on his needs-you list (brain#76).
- **#26 A push for what waits on him is recorded per entry on the account, and sent once.** (brain#77.) `RecordNeedsYouPush{resident_id, entries}` on the account: of the entries named (`%{"kind", "ref"}`, the ref as a string), the aggregate records only those it never pushed for that resident as `NeedsYouPushed{resident_id, entries}`, and an entry already pushed is no event; `Gaia.push_needs_you/3` sends one content-free push (gaia#15's, no payload) when an event was recorded, and records nothing while no key is set or no device is subscribed. The push is the same empty POST as #15's, so the device cannot tell the two causes apart: the client's notification has to be worded so it is true of both. The account stream stays out of the export (spec §6.10, `account`).
- **#27 The household contract is versioned prose, rules and settings, empty of rules until a ballot adds one; the founding witness proposes version 1.** (Design household-communication §6, Lily's, the witness's approval of 2026-11-11: the dyad contract's pattern at the household's scale, "it starts empty of rules".) On the household stream: `ProposeHouseholdContract{prose}` with a note, the founding witness's alone (`gaia_not_the_founder`), once (`gaia_household_contract_invalid`), records `HouseholdContractProposed{version: 1, prose}`; it is in force at once and is the founder's signature (`HouseholdContractSigned`). Version 1 is prose (at most 20,000 characters): no rule and no setting is seeded, and every one after comes by ballot (#29 to #31). `gaia_household_contract` (migration `20261111004710`) holds each version whole: prose, rules, settings, the bundle that made it and its ballot. `household_contract/1` answers the version in force, every version, the members with what each signed, and the ballots with their votes; members are named as the house names them, a resident by its name and a witness as whose witness it is (`Lily and Iris's witness`), never by email, because the names travel with the household's repository (sima-qian#53). The household's tables are platform-scoped as `gaia_household` is, and read only after the caller's membership has named the household.
- **#28 A member signs at join: the membership carries the version it signed.** (Design §6: "the membership event carries signed at version N".) `HouseholdWitnessJoined` and `HouseholdResidentJoined` are version 2 with `signed_version`, the version in force when the member joined (nil before any; identity upcasters, so a version 1 membership reads as unsigned), projected to `signed_version` and `signed_at` on `gaia_household_witness` and `gaia_household_resident`. A member who joined before there was a contract signs the version in force with `SignHouseholdContract{version}` (`HouseholdContractSigned{member_kind, member_id, version}`), a member's alone (`gaia_not_a_member`), of the version in force alone, idempotent. Renewal by choice, as the dyad's is, is not built.
- **#29 A ballot is a thread whose bundle is fixed when it opens, with the term its proposer sets.** (Design §6, his calls: "one or more rules or settings, voted on as a unit"; the term "a setting at creation", no platform constant.) `OpenHouseholdBallot{thread, bundle, term_days}` with a note, by any member, on a household whose contract is proposed, records `HouseholdBallotOpened{ballot, thread, bundle, term_days, closes_at}`, numbered by the stream. A bundle is one to twenty items, each `{"rule": text}` (at most 2,000 characters) or `{"setting": key, "value": text, a whole number or true/false}` (a key of lowercase letters, digits and `_`); the term is a whole number of days from 1, the proposer's, with no default (the bound of 3,650 only keeps the instant a date); otherwise `gaia_ballot_invalid`. The bundle is fixed by construction: no command changes a ballot, and a second opening on a thread that holds one, which is the only way to put another bundle there, is `gaia_ballot_fixed`. `gaia_household_ballot` holds it.
- **#30 One vote a member, a majority of the members, the whole bundle.** (Design §6: equal votes, witness or resident; majority carries; no quorum math, no secret ballots, no weights.) `VoteHouseholdBallot{ballot, vote}` is `yes` or `no`, once a member (`gaia_ballot_voted`), while the term runs (`gaia_ballot_closed` at or after `closes_at`), recorded as `HouseholdBallotVoted{member_kind, member_id, vote}` (`gaia_household_vote`). `CloseHouseholdBallot{ballot}` is refused before the term ends (`gaia_ballot_open`) and is no event once closed; it tallies over the members at the close, a member who left uncounted, and the bundle carries when the yes votes are more than half the members, absence counting as not yes. `HouseholdBallotClosed{passed, yes, no, members}` carries the outcome in words as its note. Votes are named on the contract's read: nothing is secret.
- **#31 A carried ballot is the contract's next version, whole.** `HouseholdContractAmended{version, ballot, prose, rules, settings, bundle}` is recorded with the close: the rules in force with the bundle's appended, the settings with the bundle's set, the prose unchanged, and the bundle exactly as it was fixed at the opening, never as any file says it. A failed ballot changes nothing.
- **#32 The watch is a resident's, for a term it states, and ends by itself.** (Design §4, his amendment of 2026-11-11: opt-in, temporary, per thread, never a default.) `WatchHouseholdThread{thread, hours}` by a resident of the household (`gaia_not_a_member` for a witness or a stranger), the hours 1 to 168 (`gaia_watch_invalid`; a day is the model, a week the most), records `HouseholdThreadWatched{resident_id, thread, until}` into `gaia_household_watch`. `household_watchers/3` answers the residents whose watch on a thread runs past an instant; past it the row answers nothing, so there is nothing to unsubscribe from. What a watch does is seshat#62's.

## #10 Initial vs target

Initial (P0.4): everything in #1 for one account kind, the witness, with
the API token for scripts. Target: passkeys; the community principal's
own authentication; roles beyond the witness (spec §6.1).

## #11 Depends on

The platform (the kernel, the vault, the scope). The resident system's
events, read by the witnessing handler.
