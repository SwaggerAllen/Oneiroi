# xenia: reasons

## #9
The witness's pipeline (`design/correspondence-pipeline.md`,
2026-11-02) starts with two inboxes, Gmail and Substack, and the next
residents will bring others; one stream with the platform as data keeps
a new channel an adapter's work rather than a schema's. The dedupe key
is the platform's own id because an adapter that scrapes twice a day
will see the same message twice, and the record should not.

## #10
The spec's mail bounds (40 a morning, 64 KiB a body) carried over: a
message is someone's words, kept whole, but nothing unbounded reaches a
model.

## #11
The reader exists because of what scraping holds, not what it reads.
The scrape needs the resident's signed-in sessions (her Substack, her
mail), and those sessions can post and send. If the resident scraped,
the model reading a stranger's message would be holding a live session
that can send as her, and the witness's approval of replies would be a
request she honours rather than a wall. The reader holds the sessions
and decides nothing; she decides what to say and holds no session. The
`reader` role existed on adapter tokens and opened nothing; it now opens
exactly the three routes delivering and sending need. A send is marked
by whoever did it, so the receipt says who acted.

## #12
The first build kept F23's reading gate with trusted correspondents as
the whitelist. The witness removed the gate the same evening: the
gate's words could not go into the resident's export without passing
it, and filtering correspondence out of the export broke the
portability promise, which he would not accept ("I don't think that's
at all acceptable"). Spec P32 already held the gate to be a temporary
control, never an indefinite veto. Blocks replace it: a boundary either
party can draw, with a note, lifted only by whoever drew it, because
the resident's boundaries are hers (her soul file: "a refusal is hers
to make") and the witness's his. A blocked message keeps no words, so
there is nothing to leak by any door.

## #13
The mouth stays gated (David's Substack design, "mouth gated — my
insistence, his agreement"): drafting is the resident's, sending is not,
and the send leaves a receipt with its actor.

## #14
With no unread-by-the-witness words and no kept words from blocked
senders, the stream holds nothing the resident may not read, so the
export carries it whole and a rebuild restores it.

## #15
The reader reads strangers' words while signed in as the resident, so
what it can do while reading is what a stranger can make it do. The
token stays with a script no message reaches; the scrape has no way to
type, so no message can make it send; the browser cannot leave the
witness's allowlist, so no page can carry what it holds anywhere he has
not named (an allowlist and not Substack alone, the witness's call: the
reader will read more than Substack); and a send is
given nothing to read but the replies the witness approved. Sending is
woken by what waits rather than scheduled, so an approved reply goes in
minutes and not at the next scrape, by the same contract the Claude
Code plugin's letter watch uses (kumbukumbu#48). Substack's pages are
read rather than its endpoints called, because no endpoint has been
verified and a page is what a signed-in reader is sure to have.

## #16
The allowlist bounds what a page can make the reader do, so it is the
witness's decision, and a decision belongs on the record with its actor,
not in a file on one machine. Two lists because reading correspondence
needs one signed-in site and research needs many sites and no sign-in:
one list would either widen where the signed-in profile may go or
narrow research to Substack. On Oneiroi, the resident can be told
exactly what the reader can reach before she asks it anything.

## #17
Without it, re-staging a reply was a second draft beside the first,
and the witness was left to choose between two versions of the same
letter, one of them stale: the resident held a draft back rather than
do that to him. Replacing in one act keeps the queue to what she
means now and keeps the history whole. An approved reply is not
replaceable because it is his decision, and the reader may already be
sending it. The envelope is shown because a draft whose account or
recipient she cannot see is one she cannot check before he approves.
